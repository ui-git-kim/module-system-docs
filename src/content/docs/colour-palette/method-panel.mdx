The **Method panel** makes Playground generation intelligent without over-claiming. A **Generator** chooses *how* the palette is built; five optional constraint axes each contribute an OKLCH constraint set, and a merge engine composes them into one resolved constraint that biases generation. The Generator then runs its construction algorithm *within* those constraints.

<Aside type="caution" title="Associations are conventions, not guarantees">
Colour-emotion research is mostly about what people *associate* with colours, not what colours reliably *make* people feel, and the causal "red impairs performance" line has documented replication failures. So the panel **biases** generation toward conventions — it never promises a psychological effect. UI copy reads "tends to feel…", never "makes users feel…", and every mapping carries an **evidence** tag (peer-reviewed vs convention).
</Aside>

## The Generator and five axes

The panel has two parts: a **Generator** (how the palette is constructed) and **five optional constraint axes** (what the colours should be like). The axes default to **Any** and the Generator to **Auto**; with everything on Auto/Any it rolls a varied palette, and setting a Generator or an axis (including **Culture**) biases generation. The Method panel is the **only** generation control. In the Playground it docks on the right inside the canvas — toggle it from the **palette icon** in the global header.

### Generator

The **Generator** picks the construction strategy applied *within* the merged constraints — it replaces the old Harmony axis (which now folds in as one generator). Selecting a generator reveals a **contextual sub-panel**:

| Generator | What it does | Sub-panel |
|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| **Auto** | Rolls a varied structure each generate — a mix of schemes, golden spread, distinct sets and scatter (the default) | — |
| **Harmony** | Builds from a colour-wheel scheme | A **scheme** select — Monochromatic, Analogous, Complementary, Split-complementary, Triadic, Tetradic |
| **Golden spread** | Golden-angle hue sequence — hues stay maximally spread for any count | — |
| **Distinct** | Maximally distinguishable set (best-candidate sampling) — built to be told apart | — |
| **Anchor blend** | Samples the palette along the **chords between anchor points on an OKLCH colour disc** (angle = hue, radius = lightness) — a faithful [poline](https://github.com/meodai/poline) rebuild, so chords sweep hue + lightness for curved gradients. **Each Space re-rolls the anchors** (within the resolved range) and re-samples, so a roll is a fresh poline palette shaped by the settings | A read-only **OKLCH colour-wheel** (renders the actual disc — hue by angle, lightness by radius — with the rolled anchors + the chords) + the **Poline settings**: per-axis easing (9 curves, labelled X·Hue/Light, Y·Hue/Light, Z·Chroma), **closed-loop** + **invert-lightness**. Every **Space** rolls a fresh random **3-anchor** set |
| **Hue-shift** | Hue drifts as lightness changes — the pixel-art ramp with life in highlights and shadows | — |
| **Cosine gradient** | Smooth cosine oscillation per channel (Inigo Quilez) — organic gradients | — |
| **Scatter** | Free random hues inside the constraints — anything goes | — |

Auto and the harmony schemes get a deliberate **light/dark tonal spread** most rolls; the other strategies build their own lightness structure.

The names, descriptions, order and on/off state of the generators and schemes come from the admin's [Choices tab](#choices-tab-v2530), so the list above is the built-in default. **While any colour is locked**, the roll builds around the locked colours and doesn't use the generator choice.

### Constraint axes

| Axis | What it sets | Driven by |
|------|--------------|-----------|
| **Aesthetic** | The vibe — a colour *region* (Cyberpunk, Goth, Cottagecore, Old Money/Ivy, …), grouped into six families | Per-role OKLCH **bands** (hero / accent / functional) + a selection rule |
| **Style** | The look — Pastel, Soft, Bright, Vibrant, Neon, Muted, Moody, Dark, Earthy, Elegant, Retro, Warm, Cool, Playful | Mostly **L** and **C**, soft hue window |
| **Impact** | The feel — Calm, Trustworthy, Energetic, Bold, Joyful, Serious, Luxurious, Friendly | Mostly **L** and **C** (valence × arousal), soft hue lean |
| **Purpose & Industry** | The job — Finance/Tech, Healthcare, Food, Eco, Luxury, Children, Data-viz, Illustration, Generic UI | Hue prior + L/C lean + a palette-**size** prior + a palette **structure** (hero/accent/functional counts) |
| **Culture** | The audience — China & East Asia, India & South Asia, Japan, Western, Islamic world, Ireland, Thailand, Latin America & Catholic Europe | A **soft hue lean** (hue-only) toward that culture's positive associations, seeded from the [Colour Knowledge](/colour-palette/colour-knowledge/) cultural records |

Style and Impact lean on **lightness and chroma** because those carry most of the emotional signal and replicate across cultures (Valdez & Mehrabian 1994: Pleasure ≈ 0.69·B + 0.22·S, Arousal ≈ −0.31·B + 0.60·S). Hue is weaker and more culturally variable, so axes only ever apply a **soft** hue lean — and **Culture is hue-only** for exactly that reason.

### Palette structure (from Purpose)

Beyond colour, the **Purpose** axis implies a palette *structure* — how many **hero / accent** slots the palette should have. A restrained brand (Finance, Luxury) resolves to **1 hero + 1 accent**; Data-viz to **6 heroes, no accents**; Children/Education to **4 heroes + 2 accents**, and so on. The resolved structure shows in the panel's **Resolved constraints** readout, and an **Apply structure** button restructures the current palette to those counts — adding or removing *unlocked* slots (locked colours are always kept, so the structure is a floor of the locked count) and then rolling to colour the new slots. The counts are admin-editable per Purpose in **/admin/palette-methods → Axes & priority** (a structure block on the Purpose options). It's **advisory until you click Apply** — the panel never resizes your palette behind your back.

The structure also carries a **functional** count, but it isn't applied yet — functionals are ephemeral (an unlocked one reverts to a hero on a roll), so first-class functional structure lands with the upcoming **functional-as-surface** thread. For now Apply prunes unlocked functionals so they don't inflate the hero count.

## How axes compose

Each axis value maps to a `ConstraintContribution`:

```ts
{
  L?: { min?, max?, target?, weight? },   // soft range + optional hard bound
  C?: { min?, max?, target?, weight?, relativeToCusp? },
  H?: { biasCenters?: { h, weight }[] },  // soft only
  harmonyTemplate?: SchemeKind,           // legacy: set by the scheme list, not read by the merge
  distinct?: boolean,                     // Purpose = Data-viz
  paletteSize?: { min?, max?, target? },  // advice, never forced
  rejection?: { minDeltaE?, cvdSafe? },
  aesthetic?: AestheticRegions            // Aesthetic axis only — per-role bands + rule
}
```

The **merge engine** (`resolveMethod`) composes the active axes:

- **Hard bounds intersect** across axes (tightest min/max), in priority order.
- **Soft targets weight-average** and clamp into the resolved range, fed to the generator as a pull toward the preferred L/C.
- **Conflicts resolve by priority: Aesthetic > Purpose > Style > Impact > Culture.** Aesthetic is the strongest visual-style driver; Purpose carries the hardest functional needs; Style and Impact are aesthetic biases that yield first; Culture is a soft hue-only lean and yields last. The Generator isn't in this chain — it's the construction algorithm, applied *after* the constraints merge.
- **Relax-to-soft:** if a hard intersection would be empty (e.g. Neon wants high chroma, Luxury wants low), the lower-priority bound is relaxed to a soft target rather than failing — and the relaxation is surfaced in the panel.
- **The Aesthetic axis rides alongside the merge.** Its per-role bands (hero / accent / functional) aren't squeezed into the single resolved region — they're carried in `aesthetic` and applied at generation time, so a dark base and a bright accent can coexist. See [Aesthetic](#aesthetic).

The result — including the resolved `strategy` — feeds `generateVaried` at the Playground's `regenerate()` seam, which dispatches to the chosen construction algorithm (`harmonySetColours`, golden-angle, distinct, anchor-blend, hue-shift, cosine, …). Locked colours are still respected; the soft bias only steers where inside the feasible region new colours land.

## Aesthetic

The **Aesthetic** axis constrains generation to a colour *region* that reads as a chosen vibe — **Cyberpunk**, **Retro-Futurism**, **Lunarpunk**, **Goth**, **Cottagecore**, **Minimalist / Scandinavian**, **Mid-Century Modern**, **Old Money / Ivy** — grouped in the selector into **six families** (Neon / Futurist, Dark / Romantic, Natural / Cosy, Clean / Modern, Bold / Retro-graphic, Preppy).

**Regions, not palettes.** Each aesthetic is defined as OKLCH **bands**, not fixed hexes: a **hero** (base) band, an **accent** band, and an optional **functional** band — each an L range, a C range, and one or more hue windows — plus a per-aesthetic **selection rule**. The generator samples *within* the bands, so each re-roll (Space) looks visibly different but stays on-vibe. The example hexes in the source spec are illustrative samples inside a band, never the output. The list and bands are versioned config data (`palette-aesthetics.ts`, `AESTHETIC_CONFIG_VERSION`).

**Selection rules** are part of what makes a re-roll read correctly:

- *pick-one* — choose exactly one accent hue window for the whole roll (Goth's single deep jewel; Lunarpunk's single glow).
- *warm-lead + one cool* — a warm hero with a single cool accent note (Retro-Futurism's sunset + one cyan).
- *avoid* — reject hues outside the vibe (Lunarpunk avoids red / magenta entirely).

**Where it sits.** Aesthetic emits the same `ConstraintContribution` as the other axes — a gentle L/C lean toward the dominant hero band plus soft hue centres — so it composes through the merge engine. Its real per-role bands ride **alongside** the merge (in `aesthetic`) and are applied at generation time; that split is what lets a dark base and a bright accent coexist instead of collapsing into one merged box. It now **leads the constraint-axis priority chain** (`Aesthetic > Purpose > Style > Impact`), outranking Purpose / Style / Impact. Its per-role band sampling drives the roll only when the Generator is **Auto** — any explicit Generator (a harmony scheme, Golden, Anchor blend, …) builds the palette its own way, while the aesthetic still leans hue softly through the merge.

<Aside type="caution" title="Aesthetic constrains hue — on purpose">
Unlike Style / Impact — where hue is kept *soft* because mood claims are weak — the Aesthetic axis constrains **hue** more firmly, because it encodes a *visual-style convention* ("what reads as cyberpunk"), not a mood effect. Overall muted-vs-vibrant scaling is intentionally left to the **Style** axis; the bands describe tendencies, never a locked saturation.
</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Functional colours">
An aesthetic may also define a **functional** band (e.g. Cyberpunk's cool-grey text, Old Money's cream ground). Functional colours surface as a third colour type in the Playground, *up with* hero and accent — but they're **absent by default**: they appear only once you convert a colour to functional (the swatch role menu) or load a palette that already has them. When the active aesthetic has a functional band, a re-roll recolours those functional slots from it; otherwise they're left untouched.
</Aside>

This first batch ships **8 aesthetics** spanning all six families; the rest of the ~16-entry list (Steampunk, Dark Academia, Studio Ghibli, Brutalist, Pop Art, Psychedelic, Postmodern / Memphis, Gen-Z Preppy) is staged as pure config-data follow-ups. See the [Roadmap](/colour-palette/roadmap/).

## Honesty & transparency

The panel shows, for each active axis, its "tends to feel…" description and evidence tag; the resolved L/C ranges, hue lean, generator and suggested size; and any relaxations. The palette-size prior is shown as **advice** — it never resizes the palette for you.

<Aside type="note" title="The numbers are config, not code">
Every semantic → OKLCH mapping lives in versioned JSON config (`METHOD_CONFIG_VERSION`), so ranges can be tuned, A/B-tested, or localised without code changes. The Area 3 (L,C) boxes, the warmth/weight formulas, the Valdez–Mehrabian OKLCH proxy scaling and the priority ordering are **engineering syntheses** — starting points to validate in the Playground, not established standards.
</Aside>

## Culture

**Culture is a first-class constraint axis** (since v2.33.0), modelled exactly like Impact: each culture is an option with a `ConstraintContribution`, resolved by `resolveMethod` and **editable in the Palette Methods admin** (`/admin/palette-methods`, Axes & priority tab) just like the other axes. It's **hue-only** — a *soft* hue lean toward that culture's positive associations — because cultural colour meaning is about hue.

**Since v2.39.0 the hue lean is criteria-driven by default:** with the admin's *Criteria-driven hue targets* toggle on (the default), the selected culture's lean is derived **live** from the [Colour Knowledge](/colour-palette/colour-knowledge/) cultural records at roll time — so editing those records changes generation directly, and the static per-option hue-lean editors are ignored for Culture (and Impact). With the toggle off, the static admin-tunable centres (seeded from the same band midpoints) apply, as in v2.33.0–v2.38.0.

Independently of the axis, selecting a culture still surfaces non-blocking cultural **cautions** for the palette's charged colours (a "white reads as funereal in East Asia" heads-up) — drawn live from the Colour Knowledge cultural records. Information, never a gate. (Cultures whose only association is a caution — e.g. Thailand, where purple is mourning — contribute no hue lean; selecting them just shows the caution. An admin can add a lean.)

## Choices tab (v2.53.0)

**Admin → Palette Methods → Choices** sets what people can pick in the Playground and what a new palette starts with.

- **Generators:** a switch, name, hint, description and up/down order for each one.
  - A generator that is switched off leaves the Playground picker and Auto's random mix.
  - **Auto** can't be switched off: it is the default and the fallback. Nor can the last structure Auto mixes from (Harmony, Golden spread, Distinct or Scatter).
- **Harmony schemes:** the same controls. The hue geometry of each scheme is shown but fixed. Switching every scheme off hides Harmony.
- **Colour types:** a name and plural for Hero, Accent and Functional, and how many of each a new palette starts with.
  - The renamed types show everywhere: the Playground crowns and menus, the Palette Profile, the palette sheet, the Library and the Colour Profile.
  - Hero needs at least 1.
  - **Functional stays at 0 ("Coming soon")**, because an unlocked functional turns back into a hero on the next roll. It unlocks with the functional-structure work.
  - A readout shows the result, for example "New palettes start with 3 Heroes · 2 Accents = 5 colours (allowed 2–8)". The Engine tab keeps the minimum and maximum.

**What is stored.** Save stores only what you changed in these lists. Anything missing comes back at its built-in default, so wording fixes and new generators from later module versions still appear. To hide an entry, switch it off (`"enabled": false` in the Advanced JSON); deleting it just brings it back. Unknown ids are dropped and listed as warnings when you Apply, and when the page loads a stored config that needed repair.

<Aside type="note" title="Locked colours bypass these choices">
While any colour is locked, the Playground builds around the locked colours (golden spread for one, an inferred harmony for two or more, a distinct set for Data visualisation). It doesn't use the selected generator or the switches here.
</Aside>

## Colour vision (v2.53.0)

**Data visualisation** now checks that its colours can be told apart by people with colour-vision deficiency, and any palette can turn the same check on.

**The check.** Each pair of colours is simulated as seen with protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia, and must stay at least **0.05 ΔEOK** apart.

| Type | Model | Enforced by default |
|---|---|---|
| Protanopia (red-weak) | Machado et al. 2009, full severity, linear sRGB | Yes |
| Deuteranopia (green-weak) | Machado et al. 2009, full severity, linear sRGB | Yes |
| Tritanopia (blue–yellow) | Brettel et al. 1997, linear sRGB | No: always reported, enforced only if the admin switches it on |

0.05 passes every palette designed to be colour-blind safe that was measured (Okabe–Ito, IBM, Paul Tol Vibrant and Muted, Tableau Color Blind 10) and fails the known problem palettes (Tableau 10, d3 Category10, ColorBrewer Set1). Up to about 1 in 12 men (fewer in some populations) have some red–green colour-vision deficiency; blue–yellow is rare.

**When it runs.** The check is on when the Purpose asks for it (Data visualisation does) or when the Playground's **Colour-vision safe** switch is on. The switch's starting state, the threshold and the enforced types are set in **Admin → Palette Methods → Guarantees**; the re-roll count and the largest lightness change are on **Engine**.

**How a roll gets there:**
1. The Distinct generator picks each colour so it stays apart for normal vision first, then under the simulations.
2. A repair nudges only the **lightness** of a colour that still clashes, by at most **±0.12** (admin-adjustable). It never moves a locked colour or the dark background slot, and never breaks Min adjacent contrast, Readable on background or Dark mode.
3. If the roll still fails, the Playground re-rolls (up to **8** times) and keeps the best attempt.

Smooth gradients (Anchor blend, Hue-shift, Cosine) are re-rolled but only repaired when the switch itself is on, because a nudge breaks the ramp. While the check is on, a Style's lightness lean is softened for the new colours and the locked-colour hue lean is skipped, because both squeeze colours together.

**When it can't.** Some combinations can't pass. Data visualisation with Pastel or Dark at 6 colours almost never does, because the colours are too close in lightness. The roll isn't blocked: the best attempt is kept and the status says why, for example "both are locked, so unlock one", or "try fewer colours, a wider Style or a lower threshold". Measured pass rates at 6 colours: Data visualisation alone 100%, with Vibrant 98%, Bright 89%, Muted 55%.

With **locked colours and a narrow Style** (Muted, Dark, Pastel), Data visualisation also often can't keep every colour at its usual normal-vision separation (0.12). Each Space press keeps the search short (a few milliseconds) rather than freezing the page, so the closest pair may sit a little under 0.12. Unlock a colour or widen the Style for more room.

The repair never undoes another guarantee you switched on. If **Min colour distance** is on, no pair of colours is moved closer than that distance (or closer than it already was).

**After Undo, Redo or a History restore**, the dark "background" crown and the colours the check leaves out follow the palette on screen. They come from the roll that produced that exact palette. A colour you type in yourself is always checked as a data colour.

**Where you see it:**
- **Method panel → Accessibility:** the switch, the threshold slider and a one-line status naming the closest pair.
- **Colour vision panel** (eye icon in the Playground header): the palette as each type sees it, Pass or Fail per type with the closest pair, and how the last roll got there ("Generated colours passed on attempt 2 of 8 · 1 colour adjusted"). Background and text (functional) colours are left out.
- **Palette Profile → Analytics:** the same report for a saved palette.

<Aside type="caution" title="Still being tuned">
The threshold, which types to enforce and how far the repair may move a colour are first settings, calibrated on published palettes. Expect them to change after real use.
</Aside>

## Status

- **v2.53.0**: **The Choices tab, and colour-vision-safe Data visualisation.** The admin's generator, scheme and colour-type settings now reach the Playground, through a normaliser that fills anything missing from the defaults and saves only what changed. Data visualisation now enforces a colour-vision check, shown in a new Colour vision panel and on the Palette Profile. See [Choices tab](#choices-tab-v2530) and [Colour vision](#colour-vision-v2530).
- **v2.43.0** — **Dark aesthetics get a background surface.** In **Cyberpunk / Lunarpunk / Goth**, the dark base isn't a hero — it's the **background** the neon foreground sits on. So one rolled slot is now drawn from the dark base band and **kept dark** (exempt from Style's lightness, so **Neon** brightens the *foreground* while the surface stays dark), shown as **"background"** in the Playground. Which aesthetics do this is a per-aesthetic **admin toggle** ("Base is background surface", Look tab) — automatic on the three dark ones, off elsewhere. The structural role and its dropdown are unchanged.
- **v2.42.0** — **Aesthetic accent "pops" are no longer pinned to fixed slots.** When an aesthetic themes a fresh all-hero palette, it borrows some slots to sample from the accent band (the base + pop look). That borrow now **varies its count (~30–60%) and shuffles its positions every Space press**, so the bright pops move around instead of always sitting in the same slots — and the crown on a borrowed slot now shows **Accent** (what it's playing this roll) instead of a misleading "Hero". The structural role and its dropdown are unchanged.
- **v2.41.0** — **Aesthetics now COMBINE with the other axes** (they no longer override them). Selecting an Aesthetic *and* another axis honours both: the **Aesthetic** owns hue + the base/accent look, **Style** leads saturation (Muted mutes the neons, Neon brightens them) and shares lightness, and **Impact / Purpose / Culture** lean the hue *within* the aesthetic's windows (so Cyberpunk + Finance/Tech goes cyan/blue-ward and sheds the pinks). Because the Playground is for *exploring*, the blend point **roams each Space press** — every selection always registers, but successive rolls span the intersection (dark-and-structured ↔ lighter-toward-Style ↔ bluer-toward-Purpose) so you can pick the one you like. Contradictory picks meet in the middle rather than one winning outright.
- **v2.40.0** — **Palette Methods admin, redesigned by intent.** The `/admin/palette-methods` page is re-organised around *what an admin is trying to do* rather than the config's data shape, with a **pipeline strip** naming the five generation stages. Tabs are now **Look** (every axis — Aesthetic included as a peer — on one shared **L/C region canvas** you drag, plus a **hue wheel**, with numeric sliders beneath for precise/accessible editing), **Conflicts** (Precedence + Depth + Criteria mode), **Guarantees** (the accessibility passes + the distinctness knobs, consolidated), **Engine** (rarely-touched internals), and **Advanced** (the raw JSON). The five overloaded "weights" are renamed **Limits** (hard bound) · **Bias** (soft target) · **Depth** (per-axis soft-pull, was Influence) · **Precedence** (conflict order, was Priority) · **Share** (accent emphasis). Every control shows a **reset-to-default** affordance when changed, inert controls carry an **"applies when…"** banner, and the per-option pull shows an **effective-pull** read-out.
- **v2.39.0** — **Criteria-driven hue targets** (criteria-redesign Phase 2, req 3 — the heart). The **Impact** and **Culture** axes now derive their hue lean **live from the Colour Knowledge records** by default: Impact's value maps to its psychology dimension → matching positive records → each anchor's representative hue, weighted by record strength; Culture uses its positive cultural hue bands. These **replace** the two axes' static hue centres (no matching records ⇒ no hue lean); L/C targets and hard bounds stay static, and each dynamic centre is scaled by the axis's influence weight. Admin toggle in **Axes & priority** (*Criteria-driven hue targets*, default on) restores the static leans.
- **v2.38.0** — **Per-axis influence weights** (criteria-redesign Phase 2). A new admin control (`/admin/palette-methods → Axes & priority`): a 0–1 **influence** slider per constraint axis that scales how hard that axis's *soft* pull weighs in the merge — its L/C targets and hue lean are multiplied by it (hard min/max bounds are unaffected). 1 = full strength (the default, identical to before); turn an axis down so another can lead (e.g. lower Impact so Aesthetic dominates). Optional + lenient: a config without it treats every axis as full influence.
- **v2.37.0** — **Locked colours as an implicit axis** (criteria-redesign Phase 2). When you lock colours and roll the rest, each locked colour is **analysed to its Berlin–Kay anchor** and the freshly generated colours **lean toward the nearest locked anchor's representative hue** — so the palette harmonises *toward* the locked vibe instead of golden-spreading away from it. The lean is gentle (so rolls still vary), composes across multiple locked colours (each generated colour follows its nearest locked anchor), and is skipped under an active Aesthetic (its hue windows already define the look). Neutral locks (e.g. a locked background) carry no hue, so they don't pull. Tunable via `LOCKED_VIBE_LEAN`.
- **v2.36.0** — **Functional = surface** (criteria-redesign Phase 2). A new opt-in **Readable on background** accessibility constraint: lock a functional colour as your **background** and generated heroes/accents are nudged to clear a minimum WCAG contrast ratio against it, so the palette reads on that surface (a dark surface lightens the rest, a light surface darkens them). The reference is the locked functional tagged `background`, else the darkest locked functional; with none locked it's a no-op. Toggle + ratio slider in the panel; default off, admin-configurable at /admin/palette-methods.
- **v2.35.0** — **Palette structure from Purpose** (criteria-redesign Phase 2, step 1).
