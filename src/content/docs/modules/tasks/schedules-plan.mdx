This page is the plan for schedules. It gathers what the owner asked for on 2026-11-11 while the calendar was being built, and how it is done. The owner approved it the same day. **Release S1, the tile and its marks, shipped in v1.9.0** (see [Day colours](/modules/tasks/calendars/#day-colours)) **S2, nights each, in v1.10.0** (see [Nights each](/modules/tasks/calendars/#nights-each)) **S3, schedules side by side, in v1.12.0** (see [Side by side](/modules/tasks/calendars/#side-by-side)) **S4, special occasions, in v1.14.0** (see [Repeats](/modules/tasks/calendars/#repeats) and [Day colours](/modules/tasks/calendars/#day-colours)) **and S5, a schedule in words, in v1.15.0** (see [In words](/modules/tasks/calendars/#in-words)); S6 is planned.

It builds on what calendars already do: entries that repeat and pause during a period ([Repeats](/modules/tasks/calendars/#repeats)), a colour along each day ([Day colours](/modules/tasks/calendars/#day-colours)), adding from a day, and a log of what happened.

<Aside type="note" title="The first real use, and why the plan is general">
The first use is a care arrangement: which home the children are in each night, over a year, with different patterns in term and in school holidays and set days such as Christmas. The owner wants to compare it with a second household's schedule, work out how the year divides, and write the arrangement out as proposed orders. Tasks stays general: a schedule is any entry whose calendar says what holds each day (a roster, an on-call rota, a room booking). Nothing in Tasks' code or wording says "parent", "child" or "care".
</Aside>

## Decided by the owner (2026-11-11)

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| What a schedule is | **An entry of its own kind in Structure**, with its changeovers filed under it. The owner's app already has this kind. |
| How a day shows its value | **A soft tile with a thicker line along its bottom**: a blend of the two mock-ups. The owner first asked for the line (so the day has room for other things, such as sport training), then preferred the filled days of the first mock-up, "but maybe a hybrid of both". |
| How it should feel | **Calm and simple.** "Too many calendar UIs are overwhelming with too much info." This holds for the check-in as well. |
| What a night is | **Whoever has the day when it ends.** A changeover at 3:00 pm makes that night the new home's. |
| Time with no night | **It shows as an event: a dot on the day in that person's colour**, like an appointment or training. One household's children spend five hours with the other home each Saturday and no nights. **Those hours are counted, as a second figure** beside the nights; an admin can switch that off. |
| Days and percentages | **Wanted, over a year**, kept as fields on the schedule's own kind. Structure's formulas can work from them. They feed other things, such as child support. |
| The regular schedule and special occasions | **Separate figures.** The figures are the regular schedule by itself; a tick-box works out the special occasions as well, and they show as figures of their own. |
| The year | **An admin's setting, 1 January unless changed.** |
| Orders | **A schedule has to be able to be written out as an order. Plain text for now**, to be refined later. |
| Where it shows | **On the page of the entry the schedule is filed under** (a matter's page), as well as on its own calendar. |
| More than one schedule under that entry | **The card shows the current one.** Where there is another (a proposal), a switch on the card shows that one instead. |
| Comparing | **Any number of schedules side by side** (the current one, a partner's, a proposal, a second proposal), with a tooltip on each day. |

Three mock-ups were shown to the owner: whole days filled in, a line along the bottom of each day, and the blend of the two that the plan now follows.

## The look

- **One tile, one line, one dot.** A day is a soft tile in the colour of whose day it is, with a thicker line along the bottom in the stronger shade. A day the value changes on shows both colours in the line. Anything else on the day is a single dot. The detail belongs in the day's panel, not on the tile.
- **The same tile everywhere.** The owner picked the tile from the fortnight card ("this for the day cards in calendar too"): the month's days, the fortnight card and the side-by-side rows all use it. The month adds the day's number and its dot; the small card shows the tile alone.
- **Setting a day is three choices.** The chosen day shows each value as a button, with **None** and, once set, **Clear what was set**.
- **Nothing counts down and nothing warns.** No badges, no red, no numbers on a day.
- **The starter's own components first.** A custom snippet only where no component fits, as elsewhere in Tasks.
- The owner's design references are for the feel, not for copying: quiet month grids, a line or dot per day, a day's list with a colour bar, and a few large choices instead of a form.

## What a calendar already does for a schedule

| What a schedule needs | How it is done today |
|---|---|
| A usual pattern, such as alternate weekends from Friday afternoon to Tuesday afternoon | An entry with a start and an end that repeats every fortnight and colours the days it covers |
| A different pattern in school holidays | A second repeating entry that **runs only during** the holidays, while the usual one **pauses during** them |
| The usual pattern picks up where it was after the holidays | A fortnight keeps its rhythm through a pause |
| A set day every second year | A repeat every 2 years |
| A day such as the second Sunday in May | A repeat every 12 months on the second Sunday |
| A one-off change | **Set this day by hand**, or **Change just this day** on a repeat |
| What happened at a changeover | The log: **Log this one** and **Same as last time** |
| One schedule's own calendar | A calendar that belongs to one entry |

## What is missing

| Piece | What it is |
|---|---|
| **A dot in a person's colour** | A kind's dot can take the colour of the entry one of its fields links to, so a day visit shows in the colour of the person it is with. It is an ordinary entry on the day and doesn't change whose night it is. |
| **Periods shade their days** | A kind that marks a period (school holidays, public holidays, pupil-free days) can tint its days, named in the legend. |
| **More than one line** | A second line under a day for something else that holds for the day, or a mark for an activity. |
| **Nights each** | How the days divide: beside each week, for a period (a school holiday), and for a year. |
| **Written to the schedule** | The figures go into fields an admin chooses on the schedule entry, for Structure's formulas and anything else to use. |
| **Schedules side by side** | A row for each schedule, as many as are chosen, with the nights each beside the row. A tooltip on each day says the day in words. A small mark shows where a schedule differs from the first one, for schedules of the same people. A longer stretch stacks week by week. |
| **The schedule's own side** | Each schedule says which value is its own side, so "everyone here" can be marked across schedules and one figure (a percentage) can be written for it. |
| **Special occasions** | Rules a repeat can't say yet: Easter, "whoever doesn't usually have the day", a date taken from another entry (a birthday), and different times on school days and other days. |
| **Colours from the schedule** | Where a schedule's own fields hold the colours, the line uses those instead of the value's own colour. |
| **A schedule in words** | Each of its rules written out plainly, in order, into a text field on an entry of a kind the admin chooses. |
| **A schedule at a glance** | Several months on one screen. |

## Counting

- **A night belongs to the value the day ends with.** That is the same value the line shows at the end of the day.
- **The regular schedule is counted by itself.** The usual pattern and the pattern for each kind of period decide the main figures. An admin says which kinds are special occasions; those are left out of the main figures.
- **Special occasions are worked out on request**, with a tick-box, and shown as figures of their own beside the regular ones, never mixed into them.
- **Three stretches:** the weeks on screen, each period of a chosen kind (so one school holiday can be checked for an even split), and a year.
- **A year** is the admin's choice: twelve months from any day and month (1 January, 1 July), or from the day and month of a date on the schedule entry.
- **Time within a day** can be counted in hours as a second figure, for a schedule with no nights to count.
- **The figures are a count of what the calendar shows.** They are only as right as the entries behind them, and they are not advice about what a figure means for anything else.

<Aside type="note" title="This doesn't change the no-counts rule">
Tasks never counts things left to do, days logged or days missed, and that stays. How a schedule divides is a fact about the schedule that the person asked for, shown where they look for it and written where they choose.
</Aside>

## A schedule in words

- Tasks writes each rule as a plain sentence, in a fixed order: the usual pattern, the pattern in each kind of period, then each special occasion, with its times and which years it applies.
- The result is put into a text field on an entry of a kind the admin chooses (a drafted order, say), filed with the schedule. It is a starting text, to be read and changed.
- **Tasks doesn't write legal wording.** Turning plain sentences into the numbered style of an order, with its "failing agreement" and cross-references, is for the person, for a wording template an admin keeps, or for the app's own AI where it has one. Which of those is an admin's choice.

## What an admin would set up

| Setting | Choices | Default |
|---|---|---|
| What counts as a night | The value the day ends with, or the value it starts with | Ends with |
| Count time within a day | In hours, or off | In hours |
| A year runs | From any day and month, or from the day and month of a date on the schedule | 1 January |
| Which kinds are special occasions | Per kind: counted apart from the regular schedule | Off |
| A card for the parent's page | Off, or a fortnight with its nights each, on the page of the entry a schedule is filed under | Off |
| Which schedule is the current one | A tick-box or a list on the schedule; the card shows it, with a switch to the others | The first by name |
| Where figures are written | Number fields for its own side's nights, everyone else's nights, its own side's share and the hours each way | Shown only |
| The schedule's own side | A field that names it, on the schedule or on the entry it is filed under | None |
| Periods shade their days | Per period kind | Off |
| A line's colours | The value's own colour, or fields on the schedule | The value's own |
| Lines on a day | One, or more than one | One |
| Written out as | The kind of entry and the text field it goes into | Off |

| **S2 — nights each** (shipped in v1.10.0) | The regular schedule's figures beside each week, for a period and for a year; special occasions as separate figures on request; what counts as a night; the year; the schedule's own side; written to fields on the schedule; a fortnight card for the parent's page. |
| **S3 — schedules side by side** (shipped in v1.12.0) | Any number of schedules in rows, a tooltip on each day, a mark where one differs from the first, and "everyone here" marked. |
| **S4 — special occasions** (shipped in v1.14.0) | Easter, "whoever doesn't usually have the day", a date from another entry, and school days against other days. |

Each release is checked the way the others are: the module's tests, a render into a copy of a real app with both halves type-checked, and the styling audit.

As built in v1.9.0:

- **The tile** is a soft wash of the colour of the value the day ends with, and the line sits flush along its bottom edge. A day with no value is a plain tile.
- **Up to three dots** still show on a day, with a small mark for more. The plan's "one dot" was drawn for a quiet day; a day with a visit and an appointment keeps both, each in its own colour.
- **A period outlines its days** with a dashed line along the top, named in the legend, in place of a dot on every day it covers. It is an admin's choice per period kind.
- **Setting a day** is a row of buttons where there are up to four values; more than that opens a list. Pressing the one that is set clears it.
- **A calendar's own side** is whatever value the admin's chosen field holds, read from the entry the calendar belongs to or from the entry that one is filed under. The month's answer carries it, ready for the figures in S2.
- **Colours from the schedule** need the own side: one field for it, one for every other value. A field that doesn't hold a colour is passed over, value by value.

As built in v1.10.0:

- **Off until an admin switches it on.** A calendar with a day colour shows no figures until **Show how the days divide** is on.
- **Beside each week**, each value's nights as a small square and a number. **Under the month**, the year and each period in it, opened when asked for (it reads a whole year).
- **The regular schedule everywhere, special occasions on a tick-box.** A kind an admin marked as a special occasion is left out of the figures. With the box ticked, each stretch shows a second set with them counted. A week they change has a star beside it.
- **The card counts its fortnight as it is shown.** It has no tick-box, and its figures sit beside the days themselves.
- **Hours are counted unless switched off**, for each kind that names whose time it is.
- **A year starts on any day and month**, or on the day and month of a date the schedule holds.
- **Written when the person presses the button**, never as a side effect of looking. The fields are its own side's and everyone else's: a calendar's values aren't known ahead, so a field can't be chosen for each one.
- **The current schedule** is marked by a tick-box or a list on the schedule. The card shows it first, with a switch to the others.

As built in v1.15.0:

- **Plain sentences in a fixed order**, numbered straight through: the usual pattern, the pattern during each kind of period, special occasions, time within a day, then single days still to come.
- **Shown first, saved if wanted.** **In words** on the calendar shows the text with a Copy button. Where an admin chose a kind of entry and its text field, **Save as a new …** makes one filed under the schedule and opens it.
- **A new entry each time.** The plan said the text goes "into a text field on an entry of a kind the admin chooses". It always makes a new entry, so a text someone has reworded is never written over.
- **Only the schedule's own kinds are said**: the ones that colour a day, and the ones whose time within a day counts.
- **No legal wording**, as the plan said. The sentences are there to be turned into an order by the person.

As built in v1.14.0:

- **Easter is a yearly rhythm.** A yearly repeat from a day near Easter can keep its day of Easter (Good Friday, Easter Monday) as Easter moves, by the Western or the Orthodox reckoning. With "every 2 years" it covers alternate years. This is the rule of its own that the plan said Easter needed.
- **"Whoever doesn't usually have the day" is an admin's setting on a kind.** For a kind that colours the days each entry covers, an entry left without a value gives its days to the other one than the calendar would otherwise show. As the plan said, it only means something with exactly two values; with one, or with three or more, such an entry says nothing.
- **School days against other days is "another time during a period".** A repeating entry with a time can start at another time on a day a period covers (school holidays, a pupil-free day), chosen in the repeat editor. A different pattern altogether in a period is still two entries, one pausing and one running only then.
- **A date from another entry was not built.** A birthday is already a yearly repeat on its date. Taking the date from the child's own entry would only save typing it once, so it is left until someone needs it.

As built in v1.12.0:

- **A third way to show a calendar**, beside the month and the list, where an admin switches it on. Weeks run down the page with a row for each schedule, as in the owner's spreadsheet.
- **Any number, up to eight at once.** The first row is the schedule the calendar is open for. The others filed under the same entry are shown to start with; any other schedule the person can see can be ticked, whichever space it is in.
- **A tooltip on each day, and the chosen day in words.** A tooltip needs a pointer, so choosing a day also lists what each schedule says for it.
- **A mark where a schedule differs from the first**, only between schedules that share a value.
- **"Everyone here" is marked above a day**, where every schedule's own side has the night. **"No one here"** is a second mark an admin can switch on; it wasn't in the plan and is off unless chosen.
- **Each row's nights that week**, where the calendar shows how its days divide.
- **One colour for a value in every row.** Values are named once for the whole answer, so a person who has no colour of their own gets the same calm colour in every schedule.

Found while building S2, and settled before S3 (v1.11.0):

- **Schedules share one set of periods.** A calendar that belongs to one entry read every kind from under that entry or linked to it, school holidays included, so each schedule (the current one, a proposal) would have needed its own copy. The owner chose to share them (2026-11-11). A kind can now be set to **They don't: the same ones show for every entry**. In an app with spaces, a kind a space keeps is shared inside the schedule's own space, and any other kind across everything the person can see.

## Open questions for the owner

The owner approved the plan on 2026-11-11 and asked for S1 to be built, with one correction: where the colours come from, and where a calendar's own side is named, are "too specific to be hardcoded". Both are an admin's settings (see [What an admin would set up](#what-an-admin-would-set-up)), like every other judgement call in Tasks.

Answered on 2026-11-11: the year (an admin's setting, 1 January by default), orders (plain text for now), the figures (the regular schedule, with special occasions separate), and how a changeover says who it is to (**a link to the receiving person's entry**). That is the kind of value the line already works with: the changeover kind colours each day "from each entry until the next one", with that link as its value.

Answered later the same day, before S2:

1. **Hours for time with no nights** are counted as a second figure. It is an admin's setting, on unless switched off.
2. **A parent page with more than one schedule under it** shows the current one, with a switch to see a proposal where there is one. Which schedule is the current one is an admin's setting.

Nothing is open at the moment.

## What can't be done as described

1. **Easter can't be said by a repeat today.** It moves each year, so it needs a rule of its own or dates brought in (release C6 of the [Calendars Plan](/modules/tasks/calendars-plan/)).
2. **"Whoever doesn't usually have the day" only means something with two values.** With three or more there is no "other one".
3. **A year's figures need the year's entries.** A school holiday that isn't on the calendar yet can't change a pattern, so next year's figures are right only once next year's periods are in.
4. **Calendars haven't been tried in a running app yet.** The plan put that before S2. The owner chose (2026-11-11) to update their app and try it while S2 is built, so what the try-out turns up is fixed alongside.
