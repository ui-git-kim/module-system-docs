CS1 to CS4 of the [Calendar Sync Plan](/modules/tasks/calendar-sync-plan/): Tasks puts your dated tasks (v1.22.69), your schedules' changes of hands and your journey steps (v1.22.70) on a calendar in your own Google or Microsoft 365 account, shows your own events in Tasks, read only (v1.22.71), and follows you when you move one of its events in your calendar (v1.22.72). Since v1.22.77 your [events](/modules/tasks/events/#e4-reminders-and-your-own-calendar-built-v12277) can go too, with their reminders. It needs the starter's Connections (starter 2.18.1 for Google, 2.18.3 for Microsoft 365); on an older starter there's simply no calendar sync.

<Aside type="caution" title="Checked against stand-ins, not the live services yet">
Tasks' calls to Google Calendar and Microsoft Graph were written to their published APIs and tested against stand-ins that answer the same way. They haven't yet run against a real Google or Microsoft account. Try it with a new calendar called Tasks first, so nothing mixes with your own events.
</Aside>

---

## Setting it up (you)

<Steps>

1. On the starter's **Integrations** page (Settings › Integrations), connect your Google or Microsoft 365 account, and switch **Tasks: calendar sync** on for it.
2. In Tasks' settings, under **Your calendar**, choose the calendar: **A new calendar called Tasks** (the usual) or one of yours. With more than one account ready, choose the account too.
3. **Start.** Tasks goes over everything that should be there straight away.

</Steps>

Until an account is ready, **Your calendar** says what's missing and links to the Integrations page.

## What goes out, and when

- **A task with a due date that's Time sensitive, or in a stage that means Scheduled**, becomes an **all-day event** on its due date, shown as free, with no reminder of its own.
- It **follows the task**: a new date moves the event, a new name renames it, and done, set aside, a cleared date or no longer time sensitive takes it off. A removed task's event goes too.
- A change goes out as soon as the task changes, wherever it was changed (Tasks, its page in Structure, an import). **Sync now** goes over every dated task again (up to 600 entries a run). It can run six times in a couple of minutes (with starting sync, choosing calendars to read and the all-day time), then asks you to try again in a minute or two (since v1.22.87): Google and Microsoft limit how often the whole app calls them.
- **What an event says** is its name, or just "Busy" where an admin chooses that. When an admin changes it, the events already in your calendar change at your next visit (since v1.22.86).
- Tasks only ever changes or removes events **it made**: each one is noted on its entry and kept in Tasks' own list, and marked in the event's own private data. Your own events are never touched. A copy of an entry (Structure's **Duplicate**, say) gets an event of its own, never the original's (since v1.22.86).

### Schedules' changes of hands

- Each **change of hands** of your schedules (an entry of the type a schedule's setup changes hands with, that has a Starts) goes as a **timed event** at its time, in your own time zone: to its Ends where it has one, or for a quarter of an hour.
- It follows the entry the same way a task does: a new time moves it, a new name renames it, and a removed change of hands takes its event with it. Changes of hands made with **Add to calendar** go out as they're made.
- A change of hands you add by hand counts too: what goes is every entry of that type with a time, whoever or whatever made it.

### Journey steps

- Each **step of a journey that has a date** goes out, named for the step and its journey, for example "Notes (Hearing preparation)": an all-day event for a day, or an hour from its time.
- It follows the journey: a date that moves (an anchor moved, say) moves the event, and a step that's done, set aside or skipped comes off. A step with no date yet waits.
- A step whose entry already has an event of its own (a time-sensitive task, one in a Scheduled stage, an event added to your calendar, or a change of hands) **isn't sent twice**: the step's event steps aside, and comes back if the entry's own event goes.

### Events

Events go **where you add them to your calendar**: tick **Add to my calendar** on the event (or every one goes, where an admin set the type up that way). Each goes at its times, a timed one as **busy**, with its **Reminder**, and repeating as it does where your calendar can say the repeat the same way (otherwise its first time only). The details are on [Events](/modules/tasks/events/#add-to-my-calendar).

**All-day events remind you at** (on Your calendar, since v1.22.79, shown where an admin lets events go): an all-day event's reminder comes the day before (or the days before it says) at this time. It's the app's usual time (9 am unless the admin chose another) until you choose your own; **As usual** goes back to it. Changing it brings your events in step straight away.

### Your own events in Tasks

Where an admin lets them come in, **Your calendar** has **Show in Tasks**: a switch for each of your calendars (ones you can only read, like a shared family calendar, too; not the one Tasks writes to).

- Each event in a calendar you switch on comes into Tasks as an **entry of the type the admin chose**, named as the event is, with its **Starts**, **Ends**, **All day** (the same fields every [event type](/modules/tasks/events/) has, since v1.22.74) and **From calendar**. An all-day event shows on a calendar as whole days; a timed one at its time, in your own time zone. A repeating event comes in as each of its days.
- Tasks reads from **a month back to the admin's "how far ahead"** (60 days unless changed). Older copies stay as they were.
- **How much:** up to 10 calendars, and up to 500 events on one read (at most 20 pages of one calendar). A calendar not read to the end keeps the copies it didn't reach. An event with no title comes in as "Busy".
- **What's read:** only each event's times, title, status and Tasks' own mark, never its description, guests or attachments (since v1.22.93).
- They're **copies, read only**: change or remove an event in your calendar, and the copy follows at the next read. A change you make to a copy in Tasks doesn't go back to your calendar, and is written over when the event changes. Events Tasks wrote itself are never read back.
- **When it reads:** when you switch a calendar on, on **Sync now**, and when you open the app (at most every 15 minutes). There's no notice from the calendar yet (CS5), so a change shows at the next of those.
- Switching a calendar off takes its copies out of Tasks. So does **Stop**, whichever you choose there: the events themselves stay in your calendar.
- **In an app with spaces** (a matter, say), where a space keeps the type, each calendar you switch on goes into **one of your spaces**: the one open when you switch it on, unless you choose another under **Where … goes**. Its copies are filed into that space at its top level (or under the space itself, for a type that has to sit under something), so they're yours to see and change like anything else in it. Choosing another space moves that calendar's copies there.

### Moving them in your calendar

Move an event Tasks made in your calendar (drag it to another day, say), and **it moves in Tasks too**:

- **A task's event**: its due date becomes the day you moved it to. Only the day counts: if you gave it a time in your calendar, the time stays there and Tasks keeps the day.
- **A change of hands**: its **Starts** becomes the new time; where it has an **Ends**, that follows too. A change of hands with no Ends keeps no length: making its event longer in your calendar stays your calendar's own.
- **A journey step**: the step gets the date you moved it to, as if you'd set it on the journey yourself (and only where the journey lets you change that step's date).
- **An event**: its Starts becomes the new time, and its Ends follows where it has one of its own (with How long, Tasks works Ends out again). An all-day event's days shift whole. A **repeating event** moved there (all of it, or one time) stays as moved there.

It's only the **move** that comes back: renaming an event, or removing it, changes nothing in Tasks. And it comes back **only where Tasks hasn't changed since**: if the task (or change of hands, or step) changed in Tasks as well, Tasks' date wins and goes back out to your calendar.

Moves come back on **Sync now** and when you open the app (at most every 15 minutes). Tasks looks from a month back to a year ahead.

### When the account needs you

If the connection needs reconnecting, or Tasks is switched off for the account, sync **pauses** and **Your calendar** says so, with **Open Integrations**. Nothing is lost: **Try again** catches up once it's back.

### Stopping

**Stop** asks whether to **leave the events** Tasks made, or **remove the ones Tasks made**. It works from the app in a browser you're signed in to, never from a device token (since v1.22.84). Either way your own events stay in your calendar, and the copies of them in Tasks go.

<Aside type="caution" title="Before v1.22.86, never choose remove">
From v1.22.71 to v1.22.85, **remove the ones Tasks made** also deleted the events Tasks had brought in from your calendars: your own appointments, and meetings you organise (which sends cancellations to everyone invited). Update first, or choose **Leave the events**.
</Aside>

Deleting your account leaves the events Tasks made in your calendar: the connection is closed before Tasks' part of the erasure runs. Each carries Tasks' private mark; to take them out, **Stop** with remove first.

## Setting it up (an admin)

**Admin › Tasks › General › Calendar sync**:

| Setting | What it does | Unless changed |
|---|---|---|
| **Dated tasks can go to people's calendars** | Time-sensitive and Scheduled tasks | On |
| **Schedules' changes of hands can go too** | Each change of hands, at its time | On |
| **Journey steps can go too** | Each step with a date, until it's done | On |
| **Events can go too** | Events people add to their calendar (each Event type's Add to my calendar), with their reminder | On |
| **What an event says** | Its name, or just "Busy" | Its name |
| **People's own events come in as** | The type their events come in as, read only (see below) | Nothing comes in |
| **How far ahead** | How many days ahead a read goes (30 to 365) | 60 days |
| **Moves in the calendar come back** | An event Tasks made, moved in someone's calendar, moves it in Tasks too (see above) | On |
| **All-day reminders come at** | The time of day an all-day event's reminder comes, the day before or earlier, unless someone chooses their own | 9 am |

With everything switched off and nothing coming in, nobody can sync. A switch turned off takes those events off people's calendars at their next **Sync now**.

### Letting people's own events in

<Steps>

1. Have a type for them in App Structure: one whose role is **Event** (Entry types, **What it is**), or one Tasks doesn't use for anything else. It can't be a type used for tasks or the one a schedule changes hands with. Where a space keeps it, the copies go into each person's spaces (see above); otherwise it has to be able to sit at the top level.
2. On **Calendar sync**, choose it under **People's own events come in as** (Event types are listed first). Tasks gives it four fields: **Starts**, **Ends** and **All day** (the ones every Event type has, since v1.22.74) and **From calendar**, which only Tasks writes. Tasks fills all four in from the event, and writes over them when it changes.
3. To see them on a calendar, add the type to one under **Admin › Tasks › Modes › Calendar**, with **Starts** as its start and **Ends** as its end.

</Steps>

Choosing **Nothing comes in** takes each person's copies out when they next open the app (at most every 15 minutes) or at their next **Sync now**, whichever comes first (since v1.22.94); the four fields stay on the type as its own.

Google and Microsoft 365 themselves are set up by the app's owner under Connections in the admin area (a client id and secret each). A Google app asking for calendar access has to be verified by Google before it's used beyond testing; in testing mode a connection stops after seven days.

<Aside type="note" title="Not yet">
Being told when the calendar changes (CS5, which waits for the starter's watches) is still to come; until then changes are read when the app opens and on **Sync now**. Reminders for tasks through the calendar's own notifications (Remind me) come with them; events already go with their Reminder (since v1.22.77).
</Aside>
