A **schedule** says whose time it is, over and over: who has the children which nights, who is on which shift. It is laid out on its **cycle** (a fortnight, a roster week, a summer holiday) rather than on dates, so a proposal can be worked out before anyone knows when it would start. Added in v1.22.0.

An admin sets up each **schedule type** once. Each person then lays out their own schedules of that type on their cycles.

<Aside type="note" title="What is built so far">
v1.22.0 is the admin setup: the schedule types, and the fields Tasks adds to Structure's types for them. v1.22.1 is the person's side: [laying schedules out on their cycles](#lay-out-a-schedule) and comparing them. v1.22.2 adds [holidays and special occasions](#holidays-and-special-occasions) and a schedule on real dates, and v1.22.3 [Add to calendar](#add-to-calendar). Later releases, to v1.22.47, added Save (v1.22.13), worked-out fields (v1.22.14), putting a schedule on a calendar (v1.22.16), [overrides](#overrides) (v1.22.26), [references](#references) (v1.22.27), blank dates (v1.22.28) and Print (v1.22.33), each marked with its version below. What might come after is on the [Roadmap](/modules/tasks/roadmap/#schedules-by-their-cycle). Calendars that colour their days, set up before v1.22.0, keep working and are listed under **Calendars**.
</Aside>

## What a schedule is made of

| Piece | A care arrangement | A roster | A posting schedule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The schedule**: the entry type that holds each one | Arrangement | Roster | Posting plan |
| **What sets whose time it is**: a moment (from here, someone else's time) or a block (a start, an end and whose time it is) | A changeover | A shift | A post |
| **Whose time it is**: a link to an entry, or a choice from a list | The person it goes to | The staff member, or Early, Late, Night | The type of post (Tip, Behind the scenes, Offer) |
| **References**: entry types shown beside a planner's days, only to help plan | Sports, appointments, school events | Training days | Launches, events |
| **Overrides**: other schedules that take the usual one's place on their dates (or leave them blank) | School holidays, Christmas | Leave, public holidays | Holiday periods, sale weeks |

Nothing is decided by a type's or a field's name. The admin chooses each piece.

## What schedules are for

Schedules are kept generic, so one planner serves different needs. These are the uses they are checked against whenever they change (the owner, 2026-11-15).

**A care arrangement.** What schedules were built for first. The time is one person's until it changes hands. Nights and hours each are counted, holidays and special occasions set the regular pattern aside, proposals are compared side by side, and Save records each one as it becomes an agreement.

**A staff roster.** A roster of one to four weeks that repeats, with public holidays as special occasions and leave as holidays.
- **Fits today:** a roster per person, whose time is the shift (Early, Late, Night, a list), or a roster per shift, whose time is the staff member (a link). Shifts can be blocks, with the hours in each counted.
- **Not yet:** a whole team on one roster. A schedule holds one person's time at a time, so several people working at once need a roster each. For shifts, hours each matter more than nights each.

**A posting schedule.** A week that repeats, with a moment for each post: "Fridays at 10:00, a tip post". Whose time it is, is the type of post (a list). **Add to calendar** makes an entry for each post to write.
- **Fits today:** the weekly pattern, holidays and special days that set it aside, and Add to calendar.
- **Not yet:** a post doesn't last until the next one, so the day colours, nights and hours each, **Between** and **Starts with** mean nothing for it. A schedule type for things that happen, rather than whose turn it is, would leave them out (on the [Roadmap](/modules/tasks/roadmap/#schedules-by-their-cycle)).

## Schedules and calendars

A schedule keeps its own cycle and pattern: none of it depends on a calendar. Since v1.22.16 a schedule can be **put on an entry's calendar**, and the link shows both ways.

- **Put it on a calendar**: under a schedule, **On calendars** lists the calendars it's on. **Put it on a calendar** chooses a calendar that belongs to an entry (one an admin set up under **Calendars** for matters, staff or a social account, say) and the entry whose calendar it is: the one the schedule is filed under, unless you choose another. A schedule with no start date of its own (a proposal, say) is given the date it **Starts from** on that calendar (v1.22.21): it shows from that week there, and **Change the date** moves it. **Take it off** removes it.
- **The calendar shows it as On real dates does**: each day in the colour of whoever has its night, with the line along the bottom and the changeover times, worked out from the schedule's pattern, so a change to the schedule shows there at once. A day's details name the holiday, special occasion or marked day behind it. With more than one schedule on a calendar, **Schedule** (in the calendar's **On this calendar** card) chooses which (the first unless changed).
- **Add to calendar** makes real entries of the type that sets whose time it is (a Changeover, say): events a note can be taken against. **Add to** files them under the entry whose calendar was chosen, or under the schedule. Onto a calendar, the date it starts from there stands in for a start date of its own. On a calendar that shows that type, they're its events; the day colours still come from the schedule.
- **A generic calendar**: make one calendar that belongs to an entry (Calendar, on matters), choose the types of event it shows, and put each schedule on it. It can sit on the entry's page as a widget.
- **Calendars that colour their days**, set up under **Calendars** before v1.22.0 (a care arrangement calendar, say), are separate. Removing one leaves every schedule and every entry as they are. What goes with it is what that calendar itself set up: its place under **Calendars**, its tab or widget on entries' pages, and its own settings (day colours, nights each, the schedule in words, at a glance and side by side; its flows became journeys in v1.22.48). Days set by hand on it stay as entries.

## Set up a schedule type

In **Admin › Tasks**, open **Schedules & journeys**, then **Schedules**, and choose **New schedule type**. (Schedules are set up here, not in **Entry types**, which is for types used as tasks.)

<Steps>

1. **The schedule**

   - **Its name** and **its short name** (chosen once).
   - **The schedule is a**: the entry type in Structure that holds each schedule. Tasks adds **Starts**, **Ends**, **Cycle length (days)** and **Repeats** to it when you save, so you can place them on its page (see [The fields Tasks adds](#the-fields-tasks-adds)).

2. **What sets whose time it is**

   - **It is set by**: the entry type that marks whose time it is. **Add one** makes a new type in Structure, filed under the schedule's type, with a Who field of its own if you ask for one. It is for everyone, or your own when the schedule's type is your own.
   - Tasks adds **Starts** and **Ends** to this type when you save. An entry with no end is a **moment**: from that time, the time is someone else's until the next one. An entry with an end is a **block**: its time is someone's from start to end. The person chooses each time; the admin doesn't.
   - **Whose time it is**: the field on it that names who: a link to an entry (a person), a choice from a list, or text. **Add a Who field** adds one for you.

3. **Also on the schedule**

   - **References** (v1.22.27; it replaces **Other types shown**, which never showed anything): entry types a person can add beside a schedule's days to help plan, such as sports. For each, choose the field holding its date and, if it has one, its end. A person adds any entry of these types; see [References](#references).
   - **Alternative blocks** and **Periods come from** were removed in v1.22.28 (the owner: "the alt blocks, and exceptions i think kept confusing matters"). [Overrides](#overrides) do their work: another schedule over its own dates, or a blank one. Saving a setup switches alternative blocks off; one saved before keeps them until it's next saved, and the types of period keep their Starts and Ends as their own fields.

4. **Arrangements**

   Shown once the schedule's type is chosen.

   - **Each schedule's type is in**: a list field on the schedule's type, such as Schedule Type. Its choices are your list of exceptions (v1.22.20): add a choice in Structure for each one you need (school holidays, leave, public holidays, special days, sale weeks), and say what each does: **The usual pattern**, **An exception over stretches of dates** (laid out like a holiday, over its own dates or over each period of a type) or **An exception on days each year** (a birthday, Christmas). The screens call each schedule by your choice's name. Exceptions are schedules of their own, so one set can be used by several schedules: the current arrangement and a proposal, say.
   - **Overrides need nothing set up** (v1.22.26). A person adds an override from the usual schedule itself ([Overrides](#overrides)), and Tasks keeps it in its own data. The link field an admin chose here before v1.22.26 (**Exceptions are linked by**) is still read, and kept in step.
   - **Save asks where it's at** (v1.22.13): what a person's **Save** records. **Nothing** unless you choose; **From a list field**, then **Where it's at is in**: a list field on the schedule's type other than its type field (a proposal, an agreement, and so on); or **From its stages in Structure**: the stages set up on the type's Stages tab in Structure, whose move rules then apply. Tasks' own stage templates aren't for this: they give every stage a task meaning (Doing, Done), and a type using them would show on Today.
   - **Fill in worked-out fields** (v1.22.14, off unless you switch it on): fields Tasks keeps up to date on the schedule's type, for names and formulas in Structure. See [Worked-out fields](#worked-out-fields).

5. **On the planner** (v1.22.12)

   - **Weeks start on**: Monday unless you change it. The planner and On real dates show weeks the way a calendar does, whatever day a schedule starts on. Weekends stand out: a weekday's colour is 80% of a weekend's, and a weekend with no colour has a faint wash (v1.22.18, on calendars too).
   - **Days a person can mark** (optional): names for days marked on a schedule with dates of its own, such as **Student free day** or **Staff development day**, up to 60 characters each. Add a name, rename one (days already marked keep it) or remove one. Nothing can be marked until there is a name.

6. **Several schedules**

   - **Compare up to**: how many schedules a person can see side by side. 4 unless you change it.

7. **Save**

   Tasks adds Starts and Ends to the types that need them. Anything still to sort out is listed at the top of the setup and in the list's **Needs a look** column.

</Steps>

## The fields Tasks adds

Tasks adds its fields through Structure's **field sources** (Structure 1.82 and later). They are real fields: shown in Structure's forms and on each entry's page, and read by formulas, conditions and exports.

Since Structure 1.89.11 they're also listed on each type's **Fields** tab, marked **From Tasks**, where an admin sets how they're laid out: heading, width, Links, Cards or Table, whether they're on the create form and the detail page, collapsed, and rules to show or hide them. Their type, choices, who can change them, checks and defaults stay Tasks'.

<Aside type="caution" title="Required on a schedule's Starts">
An admin can mark a field from Tasks **Required** where people fill it in. Don't do that for a schedule type's **Starts** or **Ends**: a new schedule added from Tasks has no dates until its days are set up, so Structure would refuse it. Tasks' own writes of its worked-out fields are never held up by Required.
</Aside>

On the type that sets whose time it is, and on each type of period:

| Field | Kept by | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Starts** | Tasks | When the moment is, or when the block starts |
| **Ends** | Tasks | When the block ends. Empty for a moment |
| **Who** (when you choose **Add a Who field**) | The type | Whose time it is. Handed to the type as its own straight away |

On the schedule's own type (an Arrangement, say), added in v1.22.9:

| Field | Kept by | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Starts** | Tasks | The date it starts. A date. Its days stay on their days of the week, so it can start part way through its first week (v1.22.13) |
| **Ends** | Tasks | Its last day. Empty if it carries on. A date |
| **Cycle length (days)** | Tasks | How many days its pattern covers before it starts again |
| **Repeats** | Tasks | On, its pattern starts again after its last day. Off, it runs once, from Starts to Ends |

None is filled in for a new arrangement: its days aren't set up until someone chooses them. Filled in on its page with Starts and Ends and no Cycle length, it runs once between them.

Since v1.22.12 a schedule's Starts and Ends are **dates, never times**: the times things change hands are in its pattern. Saving the schedule makes the change on a type that had date-times: each arrangement's Starts and Ends keep their day (an Ends at midnight keeps the day before, the last one it covered). Until it is saved, **Needs a look** says so, and arrangements are still written the old way.

They hold the same values as **Change the cycle**, so you can set them in either place. Put them in a section of their own with Structure's page designer, show them in lists, or use them in formulas and conditions.

- **Starts and Ends are kept by Tasks** while it runs: Structure's type editor shows them under "Fields from other modules", and an admin can move, relabel or switch them off there, but not change what they are. People fill them in as usual.
- **Who is the type's own field.** Structure locks a field a module keeps, so Tasks adds Who and hands it over at once. Set it up in Structure like any of its fields: which types it can link to, and **Only entries where…** to narrow who can be chosen. Inside a matter, the picker already offers only that matter's people.
- **A type no schedule uses any more** keeps Starts and Ends as its own fields, with every entry's times as they were.

### Worked-out fields

Added in v1.22.14; raw values since v1.22.15. With **Fill in worked-out fields** switched on, the schedule's own type gets these fields. Only Tasks sets them: nobody can type into them, admins included. They're filled in for every arrangement when you save the schedule type, and again straight after every change to an arrangement's days or pattern (Change the cycle, Start from a set-up, a changeover added or removed).

| Field | Holds |
|---|---|
| **Nights each** | A field group (v1.22.16, Structure 1.89.6): a row per person, with **Who** (a link when Who is a link), **Nights** over one cycle and **Share (%)**, whole percentages that add up to 100 |
| **Between** | The people its pattern names: a link to each when Who is a link, otherwise their names ("Alex and Jordan") |
| **Starts with** | Who has the first night of its cycle: a link when Who is a link, otherwise their name |
| **Pattern** | Its cycle in words: "2 weeks from Monday, repeating", or for one on dates of its own, "Once, 18 December 2026 to 26 January 2027" |

- **People are in the order the planner lists them** (the names above it), so the same person comes first on every schedule filed in the same place. **As usual** is never a person.
- **Special occasions** have no cycle, so theirs stay empty, as do an arrangement's whose days aren't set up.
- **Use them in Structure**: in a computed name, formulas or lists. Pick them with Structure's own field picker (their ids hold a dot). Structure works a computed name out again whenever an entry changes, so the name follows.
- **Nights each in Structure**: in a name, `{tasks.nights[0].nights}/{tasks.nights[1].nights}` gives "8/6", and `{tasks.nights[0].who->name}` the first person's name; a formula can read `{tasks.nights}` as the list of rows. Pick them with **Insert field...**. Where Structure refuses the fields, **Needs a look** says why (since v1.22.40; Tasks now needs Structure 1.89.14).
- **A renamed person** shows in Between and Starts with at once when they're links; as names, at the arrangement's next change.
- **Switched off**, the fields leave the type. The values they held stay in each entry.

## Colours

Each person (or each value) has one colour on every schedule.

- **A link to an entry** takes that entry's colour, as Structure shows it: the entry's own colour, else its type's colour for a field's value, else its type's colour. In Structure (1.88.5 or later), open the person's type, switch on **Each entry can have its own colour** on its **Basic** tab, then give each person their colour under **Colour** on their edit form or their Settings tab. The setup says so if it's off. (The older **Custom Colour** switch never did anything, and Structure 1.88.5 removed it.)
- **A choice from a list** gets a calm colour from Tasks, the same one everywhere.

## What needs a look

The setup checks itself every time it is read, and says what to do in plain words, without blocking anything:

- **Starts and Ends aren't on a type yet**: save the schedule to add them.
- **Starts and Ends on the schedule's type still take a time**: save the schedule to make them dates.
- **No field says whose time it is**: choose one, or add one.
- **"Each entry can have its own colour" is off** on the type Who links to.
- **The type that sets whose time it is can't be filed under the schedule's type**: allow it on the **Hierarchy** tab of either type in Structure.
- **A type or field it uses has gone or changed** in Structure.

Saving is refused only for something that can't work: a type that isn't there, a Who field that isn't a link, list or text, the same type as both the schedule and what sets whose time it is, a type field or **Where it's at is in** field that isn't a list (or is the type field itself), **From its stages in Structure** on a type with no stages yet, or a reference's date field that isn't a date.

## Lay out a schedule

Each person lays out their own schedules. Added in v1.22.1.

### Where

- **Schedules**, a mode on the bar at the top of every Tasks page, once an admin has set up a schedule type, unless an admin switches off **Schedules on the Tasks page** (Admin › Tasks › Modes › Schedules; v1.22.95). It shows all of the person's arrangements of a type (with more than one type, choose which).
- **A tab on the entries arrangements are filed under**, named after the schedule type: on a matter's page, say. It shows only the arrangements filed under that entry. The tab is on the entry types the schedule's type can sit under (its **Hierarchy** tab in Structure).
- **On an arrangement's own page**, as a widget an admin adds (v1.22.7). In Structure, open the schedule's type (an Arrangement, say) in the page designer and add the widget named after the schedule type. It shows that one arrangement, laid out on its cycle, with everything the Schedules page offers for it: its cycle and pattern, what it uses, its real dates and **Add to calendar** (or its special occasions or holiday rules). It shows only where it has been added.

### The list, and comparing

Every arrangement is listed with its cycle in words and what it is: its type field's own choice (as the admin named it), else **Usual**, **Stretches of dates** or **Days each year** (since v1.22.20), and where **Save** asks something, where it's at. **Open** shows one below the list. **New arrangement** makes another (a proposal, say), filed under the entry you're on. Its days aren't set up yet, and the list says so.

Tick two or more and choose **Compare the ticked ones** to see them side by side, week by week, up to the number the admin chose. A ringed day is one whose night goes to someone else than in the first; arrangements whose cycles are different lengths aren't marked against each other. **Back to one** returns to the arrangement you had open.

### Save: where it's at

Added in v1.22.13. Changes to a schedule's days and pattern keep as you make them. Where the admin has chosen what **Save** asks, **Save** is the last button in the planner's row (since v1.22.29: **Change the cycle**, **Start from a set-up**, **Add to calendar** on a usual schedule, **Add a reference**, **Print** (since v1.22.33), then **Save**); hold the pointer on it to see where the schedule is at:

- **Save** asks for its value: a choice of the admin's list field, or its stage in Structure. A proposal is saved the same way as an agreement. When it's agreed, save it again with its new value.
- With stages, only the stages Structure's move rules allow from where it is are offered.
- The list shows each schedule's value beside its name.
- Other fields can follow it in Structure. With a list field, give a field such as **Agreement date** a **Visible when** on it (the list field is not Proposal, say): it shows on the schedule's page for everything but a proposal. Fill it in there.

### The cycle

An arrangement nobody has set up yet (a new one, or one made in Structure with nothing filled in) shows **Set up its days** instead of a planner: Tasks never picks a cycle for it (v1.22.12). Once set up, **Change the cycle** changes it. Both set:

- **How long it is**: 1, 2, 3 or 4 weeks, a number of days (a 10-day roster, say), or **Once, from one date to another** (a summer holiday).
- **It starts on**: the weekday its first day falls on. Monday unless you change it.
- **It repeats**: on, its pattern starts again after its last day; off, it runs once.
- **Its start date**: optional. A proposal can have none until you know when it would start. A start date only says which week it begins in: on another day of the week, it begins part way through its first week, and every changeover stays on its weekday (v1.22.13; before, a start date moved the cycle's first day to it).
- **It ends on**: optional, for one that repeats: when it stops (Add to calendar can run until then). One that runs once has a first and a last day instead.

These are the arrangement's **Starts**, **Ends**, **Cycle length (days)** and **Repeats** fields too (v1.22.9), so whatever you set on its page in Structure shows here, and the other way round. They are dates only (v1.22.12).

**Every cycle goes by the day of the week** (v1.22.13), whatever its length. A block of dates is still dates: one that runs once from one date to another covers just those dates, and the cycle laid in it keeps its weekdays. Its days count from the first day of the week it starts in, so its planner leaves the days before its start blank, and they count for no one. **Cycle length** on its page is the days it covers.

Shortening a cycle keeps the pattern: anything past the new last day waits until the cycle is longer again.

### The pattern

The cycle shows in weeks the way a calendar does (v1.22.12): from Monday, or Sunday if the admin chose it, whatever day the cycle starts on. Days before its first and after its last are left blank.

- **One that runs once from a date** shows its real dates, with each week's first date beside the row. On real dates isn't shown under it: the planner already is its dates.
- **Any other** shows its days by number (1, 2, 3…), with Week 1, Week 2 beside the rows.

Each tile is washed in the colour of whoever has the time as the day ends (whose night it is), and its bottom line shows both colours on a day the time changes hands.

Choose a day to see what happens on it, and to add:

- **A changeover**: a time, and who the time passes to. It stays theirs until the next changeover. In a cycle that repeats, the time before the first changeover belongs to whoever the last one names (it carries round); in one that runs once, to no one.
- **A block**: a start, an end (the same day, the next day, or a few days later) and whose time it is. A block that ends the same day leaves the night where it was: it shows as a dot in that person's colour and counts as hours. One that runs past midnight changes whose night it is.

**Print** (v1.22.33, the owner: "include an export a care arrangement into maybe a pdf. So i could attach it to an email"): prints just the planner's section (its name and dates, the people, its references, its weeks and nights each) with no buttons, in a window of its own, in the light theme with its colours, scaled to fit one landscape page (A4 or Letter; v1.22.34), with no date or address along the page's edges. Choose **Save as PDF** in the browser's print window to keep it as a file to attach. The window closes by itself once you've printed or cancelled (v1.22.47). If the browser blocks the window, allow pop-ups for the site and choose **Print** again.

Each changeover and block on the chosen day has **Change** (v1.22.31): move it to another day, change its time or who it passes to, or a block's end. It stays the same changeover, rather than adding a new one and removing the old.

**Remove** takes one away. Every change saves straight away.

### Start from a set-up

**Start from a set-up**, beside **Change the cycle**, fills the days from a common set-up, so you only change what's different (v1.22.11). It works on any arrangement, regular or holidays.

| Set-up | What it fills in |
|---|---|
| **Week about** | A week each, changing over on the day of the week you choose (**Changes over on**: the cycle's first day unless you change it) |
| **Alternate weekends** | With who it starts with, except every second weekend, Friday to Monday, with the other |
| **Every weekend** | Weekdays with who it starts with, every weekend, Friday to Monday, with the other (a one-week cycle) |
| **2-2-3** | Two days, two days and three days, then the other way round the next week |
| **2-2-5-5** | Monday and Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, then five days each: Friday to Tuesday, and Wednesday to Sunday |
| **3-4-4-3** | Three days and four, then four and three the next week |
| **Split in half** | The first half of the cycle with who it starts with, the second half with the other |
| **One person throughout** | All of it with one person |
| **A copy of** another arrangement | Its days and changeovers, copied (changing either later doesn't change the other) |

- Choose **who it starts with**, **who has the other turns**, and the time the changeovers happen (**Back at** for the Monday after a weekend).
- **It replaces what's laid out now.** Change any day by hand after.
- A cycle that repeats takes the set-up's length (two weeks for most). One that runs once keeps its dates, and the set-up repeats over them: week about over a five-week Christmas, say.
- One that runs once starts with whoever's turn it would be as it begins (v1.22.12): week about changing over on Mondays, over a holiday that starts on a Friday, gives that Friday to Monday to whoever has the week before the first Monday.
- Weekend set-ups land on the weekends whatever weekday the cycle starts on.

Under the tiles: **Nights each cycle** (**Nights each over its days** for one that runs once), and **Time within a day** in hours. They're facts about the schedule, worked out from the pattern alone, so a proposal with no dates works the same.

### Who can be chosen

The choices come from the admin's **Whose time it is** field, and are the ones Structure's own form would offer for a new entry filed there:

- **A link to an entry**: you pick with Structure's own picker, given the field's own settings: the types it links to (and types built on them), the types it leaves out, its **Only entries where…** (whatever it tests, such as "picked in a matter's Focus parent field"), and the space of the entry you're on. Each keeps its own colour from Structure.
- **A list**: its typed choices, then its **Values already entered** (read through Structure, such as the people named in a matter's Focus parent and Other parent fields) and its **Records of a type**. Records chosen by a condition of their own are listed too (see below).
- **Text**: typed, with what's been entered before offered as you type.

Updated in v1.22.6. Before, Tasks read only a list's typed choices and a very simple **Only entries where…**, so a list "from values" or a link with any other condition offered no one.

**Structure works the choices out itself** (`getFieldChoices`, since Tasks v1.22.8; always since v1.22.44, as Tasks needs Structure 1.89.14), with the same code that checks a save, as for a new entry filed under the arrangement. So every choice listed (in the colours, the legend and **Each in turn**) is one Structure would accept, whatever the field's conditions, and records chosen by a condition are listed too.

<Aside type="caution">
**"Picked in" and "parent → field" tests offer no one.** Structure reads those parts of an **Only entries where…** as blank when it works out a new entry's choices, in its own picker too. To offer the people named on the entry an arrangement is filed under (a matter's **Focus parent** and **Other parent**, say), make **Who** a list whose options come from **Values already entered** on those fields instead.
</Aside>

## Holidays and special occasions

Holidays and special occasions are **arrangements of their own**, so one set can be used by several regular arrangements: the current one and a proposal. What an arrangement is comes from its type field (as the admin matched each choice). Added in v1.22.2.

### Special occasions

An arrangement whose type means **Special occasions** lists its occasions instead of a cycle. Each one has:

- **When, each year**: a date (25 December), a day around Easter (Good Friday, Easter Saturday, Sunday or Monday), or a weekday of a month (the second Sunday in May, the last Monday in May).
- **From and to**: a time on a day around it, such as the day before at 4:00 pm to the day at 2:00 pm.
- **Who has it**: one person; **each in turn, by year**, said as who has it first and in which year; or **whoever doesn't have the time** just then, which suits a few hours on a birthday.

### Holidays

An arrangement whose type is an exception over stretches of dates (a holiday) works like a regular one (v1.22.10): the same planner, its cycle laid out day by day, and **Change the cycle**. Below the planner, **When it applies** (v1.22.12):

1. **Which schedule it overrides.** It's added from the usual schedule, under its [Overrides](#overrides), and says so here (v1.22.26). Nothing else shows until it overrides one.
2. Then it depends on whether it has dates of its own.

**On dates of its own** (it runs once, from one date to another: a Christmas proposal, say), it covers just those dates, with its days as laid out. There's nothing else to choose.

- Its planner shows its real dates. To see a usual schedule's nights beside them while you plan, add it as a [reference](#references) (since v1.22.27; before, the schedule it overrides showed by itself).

**Without dates of its own** (a pattern for every school holiday, say; only for a setup that still has types of period from before v1.22.28), it follows a rule for each type of holiday the admin chose (School holidays, Summer holidays). Those holidays are entries of those types in Structure, with their Starts and Ends, entered once and shared. With Starts and Ends but a cycle that repeats, its own dates are a row too. For each, choose one:

- **Its days as laid out above**: the planner's pattern, changed by hand however you like.
- **The regular pattern carries on** (the starting choice for a type of holiday).
- **Split in half**, changing hands at a time in the middle; who has the first half can take turns by year.
- **Take turns every few days**: a week each, a fortnight each, or a few days each, from the holiday's start.
- **One person throughout**, or each in turn by year.

**As usual.** When you add a changeover or a block to a holidays arrangement's planner, tick **As usual** to leave that time to the regular schedule. A regular Saturday can carry on through the holidays this way, whatever the regular schedule says that week. Time before a holiday's first changeover is as usual too.

**Its days line up with** (for a cycle that repeats, when any choice is **Its days as laid out above**):
- **The start of each period**: the week each holiday begins in. The planner's days keep their weekdays (v1.22.13): its first day is its weekday on or before the holiday's first day. A cycle that starts on a Saturday starts with a holiday that begins on one.
- **The regular schedule**: the planner's days fall in step with the regular cycle, so its week 1 is the regular week 1. Use this with a cycle the same length as the regular one (**A copy of** the regular arrangement is a quick start). The usual nights then show as dots too.

**On real dates**, a holidays arrangement without dates of its own is shown on its own: time it leaves to the regular schedule shows as no one's. Open a regular arrangement that uses it to see them together. **Add to calendar** is done from the regular one.

### Marked days

Added in v1.22.12. On a planner with dates of its own, choose a day and **Mark this day** with one of the names the admin set up (Student free day, say). The name shows on the day, on its planner and on the real dates of every regular arrangement that uses it.

A holidays arrangement with marked days then says what they do, under **When it applies**:

- **Its days as laid out** (the starting choice): they count as holidays.
- **The regular schedule**: they go back to it, as whole days, midnight to midnight. For orders where those days aren't part of the holidays.

Marks are kept on each arrangement: a second proposal for the same holidays is marked again.

Where holidays overlap, the one that began last decides; a special occasion decides over any holiday.

### Overrides

Added in v1.22.26 (the owner: "just add another schedule to override the regular schedule. nothing about fields, or stages"). An override is another schedule of the same type that takes a usual schedule's place on the dates it covers: Christmas holidays, say. Nothing needs setting up.

Under a usual schedule, **Overrides** lists them, each with the dates it covers. **Add an override**:

- **A new schedule**: its name, and **From** and **To**. Tasks makes it beside the usual one, running once over those dates (given your type field's first exception choice, where it has one). Choose what it does: **Its own days** (lay them out on its planner) or **Leave the dates blank** (v1.22.28: its dates are no one's on the usual schedule, and counted for no one).
- **One I have**: any other schedule of the type.

It overrides from then on. Open it from the list to lay out its days; time left **As usual** follows the usual schedule. To see the usual schedule's nights beside its days while you plan, add it as a [reference](#references). **Remove** takes it off; the schedule itself stays.

References and overrides are different things: an override changes the usual schedule's dates; a reference changes nothing.

What an override covers:

- **Dates of its own** (it runs once, from one date to another): just those dates, with its days as laid out, whatever its type.
- **Rules for types of period** (an exception over stretches of dates without dates of its own): each period of those types, as under [Holidays](#holidays).
- **Days each year** (a special occasion): its days.

### Blank dates

Dates a usual schedule leaves blank are a **blank override** (v1.22.28, the owner: "it just gets created as a blank override"): **Add an override**, **A new schedule**, **Leave the dates blank**, and its From and To. Its dates are no one's on the usual schedule, and counted for no one; its own page shows just its dates, with **Change the dates**. **Left blank** (v1.22.26, types of period ticked on the usual schedule) isn't offered any more.

### References

Added in v1.22.27 (the owner: "the references are only there for helping to plan. they dont override, they arent connected"). Above a schedule's planner, **References** lists what it shows beside its days, and **Add a reference** adds one:

- **Another schedule**: any schedule of the type you can see, wherever it's filed (since v1.22.28; one filed under another entry is named with it in brackets). Whose night each day is on it, as a dot in that person's colour, from its plain pattern. It's placed by its own start date, or, without one, the date it starts from on a calendar it's on; with neither, choose a day in its week 1 (nothing is saved).
- **An entry** of the types an admin chose for references (sports, say): a dot on each day it falls on, every day of a repeating one.

They show as small dots in the corner of the planner's days, each named in the day's label. Point at a day's dots to see them named in a tooltip, each with its dot (v1.22.30). Choose a day a schedule reference covers, and **Copy schedule from** (v1.22.31, named so since v1.22.35) with that schedule's name copies its changeovers on that date into this one, as its own to change as you like; nothing stays linked. A day already the same says so. A planner with no dates of its own (a cycle that repeats, with no start date) asks for a date to **Look from** (nothing is saved). References are kept with the schedule so they're there next time, whether or not it's an override. They never change its dates, and nothing is linked to them: an override doesn't bring its references into the usual schedule.

### On real dates

Under a regular arrangement's cycle, **On real dates** shows a month at a time, its weeks starting on the admin's day. Each day's tile is washed in the colour of whoever has the night; a dashed line along the top marks a holiday, a solid one a special occasion, and each day's label says which. A marked day says its name, and each changeover shows as its time and who it passes to. Since v1.22.24 it is drawn with [the calendar kit](/modules/tasks/calendar-kit/), like the calendar: choose a day (or move with the arrow keys) and the card beside the month says whose day it is as it ends, why (a holiday or special occasion), and its changeovers. **Today** goes back to this month. It isn't shown for an arrangement that runs once on dates of its own and uses nothing else: its planner already shows those dates. Where they overlap, a special occasion wins, then an override (a holiday), then dates left blank, then the regular cycle. A blank day is no one's, and says why.

- **No start date yet?** Choose **Try it from** to see how it would fall from that date. On a calendar with a date it starts from, it starts as that date (v1.22.27). The changeovers stay on their weekdays, whatever day you try it from. Nothing is saved.
- **Nights each** for the year the month is in: the regular schedule with its holidays. Tick **Count the days each year too** to see special occasions counted in.

## Add to calendar

**Add to calendar**, beside **Change the cycle**, turns a regular arrangement's pattern into real dated entries. Added in v1.22.3.

- **What it makes**: an entry of the type that sets whose time it is (a Changeover) for each time the time changes hands, each a moment with **Starts** and **Who**, named for who and when ("Alex, 1 March 2027 9:00 am"). The first one says who has the time as the stretch begins.
- **Add to** (v1.22.16, for a schedule on a calendar): one of the calendars it's on, so the entries are filed under that calendar's entry, or **Under this schedule**. The type that sets whose time it is has to be allowed under that entry's type (its **Hierarchy** tab in Structure); the dialog says so if it isn't.
- **From**: the arrangement's start date, unless you choose another. It needs a start date and a pattern.
- **Until**: a year from then, the end of the arrangement (where it has one: the last day of one that runs once, or **It ends on** for one that repeats), or a date you choose. Up to two years, and 500 changes of hands, at once; more asks for a shorter stretch.
- **Include**: the holidays and special occasions it uses, on unless you switch it off.
- **Running it again** replaces what an earlier run made from the same arrangement, from that date on, wherever it was filed, except any entry you've changed or written on since: those stay as they are, and nothing is added over them.
- **A type that has to have more filled in** (another required field) can't be added to this way: the dialog says which field.

The entries are ordinary entries: they show on any calendar that shows their type, in Structure's lists, and on the arrangement's page, and you can open one to add a note.

## Good to know

- **The pattern lives on the arrangement** (in its data), not as one entry per changeover, so a fortnight is set out once. Real dated entries are made only by **Add to calendar**.
- **Structure 1.82 or later** is needed for the fields Tasks adds. Tasks itself needs Structure 1.89.14.
- **Your own types too.** The choices are the types for everyone, the types you built for yourself in Structure (marked "your own"; they work for you only, as in Structure), and types switched off in Structure (marked "switched off": switch one on there before its entries can be made). A type you can't find here may be a connection type, or Tasks' own task or category type, which a schedule can't use.
- **Export and import.** Schedule types are part of **Admin › Tasks › Data**. Starts and Ends are added once an import has finished, never during its practice run.
- **Removing a schedule type** leaves its schedules and their entries in Structure.
