A task that feels too big can be broken down in a few ways. In the task editor (the pencil on any task), Bite sized and the checklist are under **How it gets done**, Comes after is under **What it needs**, and Subtasks are at the bottom.

| | Bite sized pieces | Checklist | Subtasks |
|---|---|---|---|
| What one is | A task of its own, made for you in order | A few words and a tick | A task of its own, added one at a time |
| Has a size, a stage and a due date | Yes | No | Yes |
| On Today | One at a time, instead of the whole task | Its task says **Next: …** | Suggested like any task |
| Added in | v1.22.62 | v1.13.0 | v1.13.0 |

<Aside type="note" title="Checklist, not steps (v1.22.48)">
The checklist used to be called "steps". Since v1.22.48 it is labelled **Checklist** in the app, so "steps" only ever means a [journey's](/modules/tasks/journeys/) steps. (In code it is still `tasks.steps`.)
</Aside>

## Bite sized

For when a task feels overwhelming (v1.22.62; the owner: "we need bite sized jobs we can do"). **Bite sized** is in the task editor under How it gets done, on each card on Today, and beside the buttons on a task's own page in Structure. It shows wherever the task's type can hold tasks of its own.

**With AI** (an app with AI Core, and an operation chosen in Admin › Tasks):

1. Tasks asks AI Core for small, concrete pieces. It sends what the task says about itself: its name, what it sits under, its own words, its win (what done looks like, and why) and its checklist. Nothing about you goes with it: never a check-in or a feeling, and not the entry's id (so an operation can't read more of it). One person can ask ten times a minute (since v1.22.87).
2. The pieces come back as a list you can change: rewrite one, move it up or down, leave it out, or add your own. **Walk me through instead** is there too.
3. **Make these tasks** makes the ones you kept. Nothing is made from the AI's answer by itself.

**Without AI** (or when AI Core can't answer), it walks you through it, one calm question at a time:

1. "What's the very first thing you'd do?"
2. "And then?", as many times as it takes. **That's enough** moves on.
3. "Is there anything you need before you start?" **Make it the first piece**, or **Nothing, carry on**.

Then the same list to change, and **Make these tasks**.

**What happens to the pieces:**

- Each piece is a **task of its own**, filed under the task, sized light and put in a stage that means Not started, so it's in Today's pool.
- The pieces come **one after another**: each comes after the one before, so Today offers the next one and the rest wait quietly.
- While a task has pieces still to do, **Today suggests the pieces, not the whole task**.
- When the last piece is done, one quiet question asks: **Is it done too?** (**Yes, it's done** or **Not yet**).
- Nothing counts the pieces: no "2 of 5", no percentage.

## Comes after

A task can wait for others (v1.22.62): under **What it needs** (beside its decisions), **Comes after** lists the tasks it waits for, and **Choose a task it waits for** adds one (from your open tasks, the ones beside it first). Until they're all done, Today doesn't suggest it; then it joins the others by itself. This is how a task that flows on from another one works, and how Bite sized keeps its pieces in order.

- A task can't wait for itself, and Tasks won't let two tasks wait for each other (round in a circle).
- Up to 20 tasks. A task waited for that's done, or has gone, stops holding it up.
- Only Today reads it. All tasks, the Board and Now, Next, Later still show a waiting task.

## Checklist

Since v1.22.62 the checklist is something you add: **Add a checklist** under How it gets done. Once it has an item it stays open.

- Type the first small thing ("What's the first small thing?") and press Enter. Add more the same way ("And then?").
- Tick an item when it is done. The cross removes one.
- Wherever the task shows, its next item shows under it as **Next: …**.

A checklist is for small steps that belong to one go at the task (pack the bag, find the form) and stay with it. Pieces that should go to Today on their own are Bite sized's. A checklist is ticks only: Tasks never shows "2 of 5" or a percentage.

<Aside type="note" title="Where the checklist is kept">
Structure has no checklist field, so Tasks keeps the checklist on the task itself. It isn't among the task's fields on its page in Structure. To see it there, an admin can place the **Checklist** widget on that type's page: open the type in Structure, then Pages › Detail page › Add a widget…
</Aside>

## Subtasks

Where a task's type can hold tasks, the task's editor has **Subtasks** at the bottom.

- **Add a subtask…** makes a task filed under this one.
- A subtask is a task like any other. Give it a size and Today can suggest it by itself. It shows with your other tasks, with the task it is in named beside it.
- The pencil on a subtask opens it in the same panel. **Back to …** returns to its task.
- On a checklist item, the arrow (**Make it a task of its own**) turns it into a subtask and takes it off the checklist.
- Bite sized's pieces are subtasks, so while any are in Today's pool, Today offers them instead of their task.

## Setting it up (an admin)

In **Admin › Tasks › Entry types**, open an entry type (**Edit**). Under **Tasks**:

- **Checklist**: on unless switched off.
- **Subtasks**: on unless switched off. Bite sized needs it. Which types can be a subtask follows Structure's hierarchy rules.

In **Admin › Tasks › Suggest & capacity › Bite sized**:

| Setting | What it does | Unless changed |
|---|---|---|
| **Suggest pieces with** | The AI Core operation that suggests pieces, or **No AI: walk people through it** | Tasks' own operation, `tasks/bite-sized` |
| **Today offers the pieces, not the whole task** | While a task has pieces still to do, Today suggests those instead | On |
| **Pieces come one after another** | Each new piece comes after the one before, so Today offers the next one | On |

<Aside type="note" title="Tasks' own AI operation">
In an app with AI Core, Tasks seeds one operation there, `tasks/bite-sized`, bound to AI Core's **analysis** purpose (never a model). It's yours to change in AI Core's admin like any other: its prompt, its model, or switching it off. Tasks never changes it once it's there. Until the analysis purpose has a model in AI Core, people are walked through it instead. Any other task operation AI Core lists can be chosen; it's sent the task's text and asked for its pieces, which it should answer with a `pieces` list (or a line each).
</Aside>

### Letting a task hold subtasks

Whether one entry can be filed under another is Structure's rule, set on each type.

- **A fresh app** (v1.22.62 on): Tasks' own **Task** type holds tasks of its own from the start. Nothing to do.
- **An app set up before v1.22.62**: Tasks' own **Task** type was seeded holding nothing ("Maximum children per node" at 0). Under **Subtasks**, Entry types says so and offers **Let it**. That changes just that one rule on the type, in Structure, and nothing else (the owner asked "we cant update the tasks type?"). It's only ever offered for Tasks' own type, and only when an admin asks.
- **Any other type**: change it in Structure.

<Steps>

1. In Structure's **App Structure**, open the type.
2. On the **Hierarchy** tab, clear **Maximum children per node** if it's 0.
3. If **Can contain** lists types, add the type itself. Left empty, it allows any.
4. Save.

</Steps>

Once the type allows it, **Subtasks** and **Bite sized** appear in the task editor, and the **Tasks** tab appears on a task's own page in Structure.

## Good to know

- **Up to 12 pieces** in one go of Bite sized. A piece can be broken down again later.
- **Up to 50 items** on one checklist.
- **A checklist item's words can't be changed yet**, and items can't be moved. Remove one and add it again.
- **Switching the checklist off for a type** hides its items without deleting them. They come back with the setting.
- **A subtask can be of another type** where Structure's rules allow that type under the task's type. Adding one makes the task's own type where it can.
