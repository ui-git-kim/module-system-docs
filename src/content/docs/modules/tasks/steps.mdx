A task that feels too big can be broken down two ways: a **checklist of steps** kept on the task, and **subtasks**, which are tasks of their own filed under it. Added in v1.13.0.

| | Steps | Subtasks |
|---|---|---|
| What one is | A few words and a tick | A task of its own |
| Has a size, a stage and a due date | No | Yes |
| On Today, All tasks and the Board | Its task says **Next: …** | Shows as a task, with the task it is in named beside it |
| Needs anything changed in Structure | No | The kind has to be allowed to hold its own kind |

---

## Steps

Open a task (the pencil on any task). **Steps** is under **Size**.

- Type the first small step and press Enter. Add more the same way.
- Tick a step when it is done. The cross removes one.
- Wherever the task shows, its next step shows under it as **Next: …**.

Steps are ticks only. Tasks never shows "2 of 5" or a percentage, and a step that isn't ticked is just a step.

<Aside type="note" title="Where steps are kept">
Structure has no checklist field, so Tasks keeps the steps on the task itself. They aren't among the task's fields on its page in Structure. To see them there, an admin can place the **Steps** widget on that kind's page: open the type in Structure, then Pages › Detail page › Add a widget…
</Aside>

---

## Subtasks

Where a task's kind can hold tasks, the task's editor has **Subtasks** at the bottom.

- **Add a subtask…** makes a task filed under this one.
- A subtask is a task like any other. Give it a size and Today can suggest it by itself. It shows with your other tasks, with the task it is in named beside it.
- The pencil on a subtask opens it in the same panel. **Back to …** returns to its task.
- On a step, the arrow (**Make it a task of its own**) turns the step into a subtask and takes it off the checklist.
- Finishing a task doesn't wait on its subtasks, and nothing counts them.

---

## Setting it up (an admin)

In **Admin › Tasks**, open a kind of entry. Under **Tasks**:

- **A checklist of steps**: on unless switched off.
- **Subtasks**: on unless switched off. Which kinds can be a subtask follows Structure's hierarchy rules.

### Letting a task hold subtasks

Whether one entry can be filed under another is Structure's rule, set on each type. Tasks' own **Task** type starts out holding nothing, so it has no subtasks until that is changed:

<Steps>

1. In Structure's **App Structure**, open the type (Task).
2. On the **Hierarchy** tab, clear **Maximum children per node**. It is 0 to begin with.
3. If **Can contain** lists kinds, add the type itself. Left empty, it allows any.
4. Save.

</Steps>

Under **Subtasks**, Admin › Tasks says what is stopping a kind when something is. Once the type allows it, **Subtasks** appears in the task editor, and the **Tasks** tab appears on a task's own page in Structure.

<Aside type="note" title="Why Tasks doesn't change this for you">
A type's hierarchy belongs to Structure, and it decides more than subtasks. Tasks reads the rule and says what it found; changing it stays an admin's own step in Structure.
</Aside>

## Good to know

- **Up to 50 steps** on one checklist. Something bigger may sit better as subtasks.
- **A step's words can't be changed yet.** Remove it and add it again.
- **Steps can't be moved up or down yet.** They stay in the order they were added.
- **Switching the checklist off for a kind** hides its steps without deleting them. They come back with the setting.
- **A subtask can be of another kind** where Structure's rules allow that kind under the task's kind. Adding one makes the task's own kind where it can.
- **Today's choice of tasks hasn't changed.** A task shows its next step; a subtask with a size is suggested like any task.
