A calendar shows chosen kinds of Structure entry as a month: a dot on each day for each entry, a legend of groups, and the chosen day's entries underneath. An admin makes one in **Admin › Tasks**, in the same editor as a timeline. Added in v1.4.0.

<Aside type="note" title="What it does so far">
The month, dots, groups, the day's entries and a list (v1.4.0), times in another time zone (v1.4.1), [entries that repeat](#repeats) (v1.5.0), [a colour for each day](#day-colours) (v1.6.0), [adding an entry from a day](#add-from-the-calendar) (v1.7.0) and [logging what happened](#logging-what-happened) (v1.8.0). What comes next is set out in the [Calendars Plan](/modules/tasks/calendars-plan/#releases). An entry's own details are still changed on its page in Structure.
</Aside>

---

## What a kind of entry needs

The same as for a timeline:

- **A date or date-time field**, which is the day it sits on. A second one can be its end, for an entry that runs over several days.
- **To be switched on in Structure**, and not a connection type or one of Tasks' own kinds.
- An app-level kind works for everyone, each seeing their own entries. A kind you built for yourself works for you only.

---

## Make the calendar

In **Admin › Tasks**, go to the **Timelines** card and choose **New timeline**. A calendar is a timeline with **A calendar** ticked.

<Steps>

1. **Name**

   What people see in the list of calendars, on its tab and on its widget.

2. **What it shows**

   Up to eight kinds of entry. For each one:

   - **Its date**: the date or date-time field that places it.
   - **It ends**: another date field, for an entry that runs over several days. Leave it at **On the same day** otherwise.
   - **Its times are in**: for a date that has a time, the time zone it was recorded in. See [time zones](/modules/tasks/timelines/#time-zones).
   - **Title**, **Summary**, **Show**, **Called**: as for a timeline.
   - **Colour**: the dot's colour. Its colour in Structure (the entry's own, or its type's), its stage's colour, **the colour of an entry it links to** (v1.9.0: a visit in the colour of the person it is with), or none.
   - **Its group on a calendar**: kinds given the same group share one line in the legend. Leave it empty and the kind is its own group.
   - **People can add one from a calendar**: on unless you switch it off. See [Add from the calendar](#add-from-the-calendar).
   - **Its entries can repeat**: people can make their own entries of this kind repeat, from the calendar. Off unless you switch it on. See [Repeats](#repeats).
   - **What happened is logged as**: another of the calendar's kinds that records what happened on each of this one's days. See [Logging what happened](#logging-what-happened).
   - **It marks a period**: for a kind such as school holidays. A repeat on this calendar can pause while one of its entries covers the day, or run only then. **It outlines its days on a calendar** (v1.9.0) then draws a dashed line along the top of each day it covers, in place of a dot on every day.
   - **It colours each day of a calendar**: its entries set the colour along each day, from each entry until the next one, or for the days each entry covers. See [Day colours](#day-colours).

3. **Where it shows**

   - **Everything each person can see**, or **One entry's own** (a matter's dates, say), as for a timeline.
   - **Show it as**: tick **A calendar**. Keep **A timeline** ticked as well and people can switch between the two.
   - **The week starts on** Monday or Sunday.
   - **People can hide a group**: lets people switch a group off from the legend.
   - **A repeat that ends after a number of times counts**: every day it would fall on (as most calendar apps count), or only the days it happens. Shown once a kind can repeat.
   - **What a day's colour shows**, **A day the value changes on shows** and **People can set a day by hand**: shown once a kind colours the days. See [Day colours](#day-colours).
   - **List it in Tasks**: lists it under **Calendar**.
   - For one entry's own: **Show it as a tab** on each entry, and **A widget for the entry's page**, where **The month** puts the calendar itself on the page.

4. **Preview and save**

   The preview shows your own entries as a list. Saving switches each kind on for Timelines, as for any timeline.

</Steps>

## Where people find it

- **Calendar** in the view switcher at the top of every Tasks page. It appears once there is a calendar listed in Tasks. With more than one, a row of names chooses between them.
- **A tab on each entry it belongs to.** Where the view is both a timeline and a calendar, a switch on the tab chooses.
- **A widget on the entry's page.** Set the widget to **The month**, then place it in Structure: open the type, then **Pages › Detail page › Add a widget…**.
- **In the sidebar**, when an admin has chosen a Tasks section of links and ticks Calendar (Admin › Tasks › Sidebar).

---

## Reading the calendar

- **Dots.** Each entry is a dot on its day, in its colour. An entry with an end has a dot on every day it covers. Up to three dots show on a day; more than that shows a small mark, never a number.
- **The legend** names each group with its colour. Where the admin allows it, tapping a group hides or shows it.
- **The day.** Choose a day to see its entries underneath, in time order, each opening its own page in Structure. An entry that runs over several days says where the day sits in it ("Since Sat 27 Jun, until Sun 12 Jul").
- **Month and List.** **List** shows the same weeks as a list.
- **Moving about.** The arrows change the month and **Today** comes back. On a keyboard, the arrow keys move from day to day.
- **Today** is marked. Nothing is coloured for lateness, and a day that has passed looks like any other.

## Add from the calendar

Choose a day, then **Add**, beside the day's date. Added in v1.7.0.

<Steps>

1. **What to add**: one of the calendar's kinds (asked only when there is more than one).
2. **Title**, and where the kind has them: its **Time**, when **It ends**, and the value it gives a day (who it is to, say).
3. **Add** keeps you on the calendar. **Add and open** goes to the new entry's own page, for everything else about it.

</Steps>

The entry is an ordinary entry in Structure, made as you. It shows on its day straight away, and you can make it repeat from there.

### Which kinds can be added this way

| The kind | What Add does |
|---|---|
| Has to have only a title and its date, plus anything the calendar already maps (an end, a title field, the value that colours a day) | The short form makes it |
| Has to have something more (another field marked as required in Structure) | The form says so and links to that kind's own form in Structure. See the note below |
| Only an admin may make one, or the admin switched adding off for it | It isn't offered |

<Aside type="caution" title="A kind that needs more: the date isn't carried over yet">
Structure's own form can be opened from a link, but it can't be handed the day you chose, and on a calendar that belongs to one entry it can't be told where the new entry belongs. So for those kinds you choose the date again in Structure's form. A request to Structure to close this is with the owner.
</Aside>

### Good to know about adding

- **An admin's switch.** **People can add one from a calendar** is on for each kind until an admin switches it off in the editor's **What it shows** step.
- **Where it goes.** On a calendar that belongs to one entry (a matter), the new entry is filed under that entry, or linked to it through the kind's own field, the same way the calendar finds its entries.
- **Times** are written as you type them. Where a kind's times are in a set time zone, the form says which.
- **The value that colours a day** offers your own entries of the kinds it can point at (up to 30), or every choice of a list.
- **A name already used** under the same parent gets "(2)", as elsewhere in Tasks.
- **Structure has the last word.** If it refuses the entry, the form shows its reason.

## Repeats

An entry can repeat: a handover every second Friday, a class every Tuesday and Thursday, a review on the first Monday of each month. It stays one entry. The calendar works out the days it falls on, so there is nothing to copy forward and nothing to tidy up. Added in v1.5.0.

### Switch it on (an admin)

In the editor's **What it shows** step:

- **Its entries can repeat** on each kind that should be able to.
- **It marks a period** on a kind whose entries are stretches of days a repeat should follow, such as school holidays. An entry with no end is a period of one day.

In **Where it shows**, **A repeat that ends after a number of times counts** chooses what "ends after 10 times" means: **every day it would fall on** (a day it pauses or leaves out still uses one up), or **only the days it happens**.

### Make an entry repeat

<Steps>

1. Add the entry in Structure as usual, with its date (and time).
2. On the calendar, choose its day. Under your own entry there is **Repeat**.
3. Answer the questions, top to bottom. The sentence under the title says the repeat back as you choose: "Every fortnight on Friday, pausing during school holidays."
4. **Save.** The entry now shows on each of its days.

</Steps>

What you can choose:

| Question | Choices |
|---|---|
| **It repeats** | Every day, week, fortnight, month or year, or **Another rhythm** (every 3 weeks, every 6 months) |
| **On** (weekly rhythms) | The days of the week. Its own day is always one of them |
| **Each month** (monthly rhythms) | On its date (the 17th), on its weekday of the month (the third Friday), and, where the entry is on one, the last such weekday or the last day of the month |
| **It ends** | Never, on a day, or after a number of times |
| **During** each kind of period | It carries on as usual, it pauses, or it runs only then |

### Change it later

Under one of a repeating entry's days:

- **Change how it repeats** opens the same questions. Opened from one of its later days, it asks which days to change: **that day and the days after** (what has already been stays as it was), or **every day of it**. **Stop repeating** is there too.
- **Change just this day** makes that one day an entry of its own and opens it, so you can change its time or anything else. The repeat leaves that day out.
- **Skip this day** leaves one day out. **Put it back** appears straight away, and the editor lists the days left out with **Put back** beside each.

### What you'll see

- **On the calendar**, a dot on each of its days, and its rhythm on the day's card ("Every fortnight on Friday").
- **On a timeline**, the entry once, on its first day, with its rhythm.
- **On the widgets** ("What's coming up" and "The latest"), each of its days among the other entries.

### Good to know about repeats

- **Only your own entries.** You can change how an entry repeats when it is yours; everyone who can see it sees its days.
- **A fortnight keeps its rhythm.** Which week is "on" is set by the entry's first day. A pause or a skipped day never shifts it.
- **A month without that day is passed over.** A repeat on the 31st doesn't run in a 30-day month.
- **Periods are the calendar's own.** A repeat pauses for the periods this calendar shows. On a calendar belonging to one entry (a matter), those are that entry's own periods.
- **A period can repeat too** (a public holiday on the same date each year), when its kind has **Its entries can repeat** switched on.
- **"That day and the days after" makes a second entry.** The first entry keeps its days up to the day before, and a copy of it carries the new repeat from that day. Under a parent entry, where two entries can't share a name, the copy is named with "(2)".
- **Times.** Each day is at the same clock time, in the time zone the kind's times are recorded in, so daylight saving never moves it. An entry that runs over several days keeps its length on each of its days.
- **Limits.** One entry shows up to 500 days in one answer, a repeat can leave out up to 366 single days, and can follow up to three kinds of period.

## Logging what happened

What was planned and what happened are kept apart. A handover is scheduled for 5:00 pm every second Friday; what happened on one Friday (it ran late, who was there, a note) is its own entry, a log, linked back to the handover. Added in v1.8.0.

### Switch it on (an admin)

<Steps>

1. In Structure, make the kind that records what happened: a date (or date and time), a link back to the scheduled kind, and whatever else you want to record (how it went, times, notes).
2. In the calendar's editor, add both kinds under **What it shows**.
3. On the scheduled kind, set **What happened is logged as** to the log kind, and check **The link from a log back to it**.
4. **A day can be logged before it comes** is off unless you switch it on.

</Steps>

### Log a day

Choose the day. Under the entry:

- **Log this one** makes the log, linked to the entry and dated that day at the entry's own time, and opens it so you can fill in what happened.
- **Same as last time** makes one with everything the most recent earlier log said, for the days that went as usual. It shows once there is an earlier log.

The log then sits under the entry on that day, and opens its own page. A day that is logged already doesn't offer the buttons again.

### What you'll see

- **On the calendar**, the log under its entry on the chosen day, marked "What happened". It doesn't add a dot of its own.
- **On a timeline** and in the calendar's **List**, each log as an entry in its own right.
- **A day with no log is just a day.** Nothing is marked as missed, and nothing counts how many are logged.

<Aside type="caution" title="Log this one opens the log after making it">
The plan was for **Log this one** to open the log's own form with the day already filled in. Structure's form can't be handed starting values yet, so Tasks makes the log first and then opens it. That works where the log kind has no other field marked as required. Where it has one, **Log this one** isn't offered, and **Same as last time** is (once one log exists, made on its own page). The request to Structure that would close this is with the owner.
</Aside>

### Good to know about logs

- **Which day a log belongs to** is its own date. Change that date and it moves: it then shows as an entry on the new day instead of under the scheduled one.
- **Where it goes.** On a calendar that belongs to one entry, a log is filed where that calendar's entries belong.
- **A day a repeat leaves out or pauses** can't be logged, because the entry doesn't fall on it.
- **Any kind can have a log**, not only one that repeats.

## Day colours

A bar along the bottom of each day, in the colour of a value the day carries: who has care, where you're working, which roster is on. An admin chooses which kinds of entry set it. Added in v1.6.0.

### Switch it on (an admin)

In the editor's **What it shows** step, for each kind that should set the colour, choose **It colours each day of a calendar**:

| Choice | For | How a day gets its value |
|---|---|---|
| **From each entry until the next one** | An entry that marks a change, such as a handover | The value stands from the entry's day (and time) until the next entry changes it |
| **For the days each entry covers** | An entry with a start and an end, such as a block of days | The value stands for those days. An entry with no end covers its own day |

Then choose **The value**: the field that holds it. It can be a link to one entry (a person, say), a list, or a text field. For "until the next one" there is also **What it was before**, which is optional and only used for [a quiet note](#reading-it).

Both ways can be used on one calendar. Where more than one applies to a day, the order is: a day set by hand, then an entry that covers the day (the one that started latest), then the latest entry whose value holds.

In **Where it shows**:

- **What a day's colour shows**: a few words for the legend, such as "Who has care".
- **A day the value changes on shows**: both colours, before and after, or only the colour it ends the day with.
- **People can set a day by hand**: No, or the kind of entry it makes. It has to be a kind that colours the days each entry covers, with dates that have no time.

For a calendar that belongs to one entry (v1.9.0):

- **Its own side is named**: which value is this calendar's own side (the person whose calendar it is, say). Nowhere, by a field on the entry the calendar belongs to, or by a field on the entry that one is filed under. Nothing is assumed from a field's name.
- **Its own side's colour** and **Every other value's colour**: text fields on the entry the calendar belongs to that hold a colour such as #3366cc. They are used once the own side is named, and replace each value's own colour.

### Colours

- A value that links to an entry takes that entry's colour in Structure: its own, or its type's colour rules.
- Anything else gets a calm colour from Tasks, kept the same for that value across the calendar.
- To choose a colour, give the entry one in Structure.

Colour is never the only signal. The legend names each value, each day's label says it, and so does the chosen day.

### Reading it

- **The tile and its line** (v1.9.0). Each day is washed softly in the colour of the value it ends with, with a stronger line along its bottom edge. On a day the value changes, the line shows both colours (before and after), unless the admin chose to show only the one it ends with.
- **The legend** names what the colour shows and each value on screen.
- **The chosen day** says it in words: "Who has care: Alex until 3:00 pm, then Jordan". An entry with no time changes the value at some point that day, so no time is said.
- **A quiet note.** Where an entry says what the value was before it, and the calendar shows something else, the day says so in a plain line ("“Friday swap” says it was Alex before. The calendar shows Jordan."). It is a note to look at, never a warning.
- **Repeats feed it.** A repeating entry sets the value on each of its days, and a day it pauses or leaves out no longer changes it.

### Set a day by hand

Where the admin allows it:

Choose the day. Each value is a button, with **None** for a day that should show nothing: press one and the day is set (v1.9.0). Where there are more than four values to choose from, **Set this day by hand** opens a list instead.

That makes a one-day entry of the kind the admin chose, named for the value and the day. It is an entry like any other: open it to add a note about why. It wins over everything else on that day. **Clear what was set** (or pressing the button that is set) removes it, and the day goes back to what the other entries say.

### Good to know about day colours

- **The choices offered.** For a list, every choice. For a link to an entry, the entries the days on screen already show; to use someone else, add the entry on its own page.
- **Where the entry goes.** On a calendar that belongs to one entry (a matter), a day set by hand is filed under that entry.
- **A kind with other fields that are required** can't be used for setting a day by hand: Structure refuses an entry without them. Use a simple kind: a date, the value, and room for a note.
- **An entry you can't open** still colours its days, without its name.
- **Limits.** One answer names up to 60 values.

## Nights each

On a calendar with a day colour, an admin can show how the days divide: the nights each value has. Added in v1.10.0, as release S2 of the [Schedules Plan](/modules/tasks/schedules-plan/). It is a fact about the schedule, shown only where an admin switches it on. Tasks still never counts things to do.

### Switch it on (an admin)

In the calendar's editor, under **Where it shows**, once a kind colours the days:

- **Show how the days divide**: off until it is switched on.
- **A night belongs to**: the value the day ends with (a changeover at 3:00 pm makes that night the new value's), or the value the day starts with. Each day's tile is washed in that value's colour.
- **Count time within a day, in hours**: on unless switched off. It adds a second figure for each kind that says whose time it is (see below).
- **The day a year starts on** and **The month a year starts in**: 1 January unless changed. For a calendar that belongs to one entry, **Or from a date the entry holds** takes the day and month from a date on that entry (the day an arrangement began, say).

On a kind:

- **It is a special occasion**, on a kind that colours days or whose time is counted: its entries are left out of the regular schedule and counted in figures of their own.
- **Its time within a day counts for**, on a kind whose date and end both have a time (a visit, say): the field that names who the time is with. Each entry's hours are added up for that value. It changes no night.

### Reading it

- **Beside each week**: a small square in each value's colour, with its nights that week. The week's label says it in words.
- **Under the month**: **Nights each**. **Show the year** opens the year the month falls in, with each value's nights and its share and any hours within a day. Each period in the year (a school holiday) is listed the same way, so one holiday can be checked for an even split.
- **Special occasions.** The figures are the regular schedule. Tick **Count special occasions too**, and each stretch also shows the figures with them counted, as a second set. A week that special occasions change has a small star beside it until the box is ticked.
- **A night with no value** isn't given to anyone. Where a stretch has some, a line says each share is of the nights that have a value.

### Write the figures to the entry

For a calendar that belongs to one entry, an admin can choose number fields on that entry for a year's figures, under **Write a year's figures to the entry**: its own side's nights, everyone else's nights, its own side's share, and the hours within a day each way. Each can be chosen once for the regular schedule and once with special occasions. It needs **Its own side is named** to be set first.

People then press **Write the year's figures to the entry**, under the year. The figures go into those fields, where Structure's formulas (and anything else) can use them. They are written when the button is pressed and not kept up to date by themselves: press it again after the schedule changes.

### A card for the page above

Where a calendar's entry is filed under another entry (a schedule under a matter), **A card for the page of the entry it is filed under** offers a card for that entry's page: this week and the next as day tiles, with the nights each where the figures are on. Place it on the type's page in Structure like any widget.

Where more than one is filed there (a current one and a proposal), the card shows the current one and has a switch to the others. **The current one is marked by** names the tick-box or list on the calendar's own entry that says which it is. With nothing chosen, the first by name shows.

### Good to know about nights each

- **The figures count what the calendar shows.** They are only as right as the entries behind them, and a year is complete only once its periods (next year's school holidays) are on the calendar.
- **The card counts its fortnight as it is shown**, special occasions included.
- **A period that runs past the end of the year** is counted whole.
- **A period every calendar shares.** On a calendar that belongs to one entry, each kind is read from under that entry unless an admin chooses otherwise. For school holidays that several schedules share, set the kind to **They don't: the same ones show for every entry** (under **Where it shows**, v1.11.0). Add each holiday once, and every schedule's calendar pauses for it and counts it.
- **Not advice.** The figures say how the calendar divides, and nothing about what that means for anything else.

## Good to know

- **Times in another time zone** show at your own time, with the recorded time beside it, once the kind has its time zone set (v1.4.1). A kind with none shows its times as recorded.
- **An entry's own details are changed on its page.** From the calendar you can add an entry, set how it repeats and set a day's colour by hand; its date, title and other fields are changed on its own page, which each entry on the calendar opens.
- **A very full stretch.** If a month holds more than the calendar reads at once, a line under it says so; the list on a timeline pages through everything.
- **In an app with spaces**, a calendar over "everything each person can see" shows the open space's entries. For one matter's dates, use **One entry's own**.

## Example: a matter's dates

<Steps>

1. **New timeline**, named "Dates".
2. **What it shows**: Court events (the court date), in the group "Court"; Appointments (the appointment's date and time), in the group "Children".
3. **Where it shows**: **One entry's own**, belonging to each **Matter**. Tick **A calendar**. Set the widget to **The month**.
4. Save, then place the "Dates" widget on the Matter type's page in Structure.

</Steps>

Each matter now has a **Dates** tab and, once placed, its calendar on the matter's own page.

## Example: every second Friday, except in the holidays

<Steps>

1. In the calendar's editor, switch on **Its entries can repeat** for the kind that holds the regular event, and **It marks a period** for the kind that holds the holidays. Save.
2. Add each holiday as an entry of the period kind, with its first and last day.
3. Add the regular event once, on its first Friday, with its time.
4. On the calendar, choose that Friday, then **Repeat**: **Every fortnight**, and **During** the holidays, **It pauses**. Save.

</Steps>

The event now shows every second Friday and leaves out any that fall in a holiday. For something that happens only in the holidays, add a second entry and choose **It runs only then**.

## Example: who has care

Say the app has a **Person** kind, and a **Handover** kind with a date and time, a link **To** a person and a link **From** a person.

<Steps>

1. In the calendar's editor, for Handovers choose **It colours each day of a calendar**: **From each entry until the next one**, with **The value** set to **To** and **What it was before** set to **From**. Switch on **Its entries can repeat** as well.
2. Add a simple kind for days set by hand (a date and a link to a person), choose **For the days each entry covers** for it, and pick it under **People can set a day by hand**.
3. Set **What a day's colour shows** to "Who has care". Save.
4. Give each person a colour in Structure.
5. Add the regular handovers once each and make them repeat: one to each person, every fortnight.

</Steps>

Each day now carries the colour of the person who has care, a handover day shows both, and a day that went differently can be set by hand. To see how a week, a holiday and the year divide, switch on **Show how the days divide** (see [Nights each](#nights-each)).
